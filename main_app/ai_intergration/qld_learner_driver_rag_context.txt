PART 1: LICENSING PROCEDURES
================================================================================

MINIMUM AGE REQUIREMENTS
- Minimum age to apply for learner licence: 16 years old
- Learner licence valid for: 3 years from issue date
- Can apply for provisional (P1) licence from: 17 years old

STEPS TO OBTAIN LEARNER LICENCE (Class C - Standard Cars)

Step 1: Complete pre-learner training requirement
- Option A: Complete PrepL online interactive program (can start at 15 years 11 months)
- Option B: Pass written road rules knowledge test at TMR (Transport and Main Roads) customer service centre
- PrepL recommended as it focuses on safe driving behaviours and attitudes

Step 2: Gather required proof of identity
- Must provide documentary proof of identity
- Acceptable documents: birth certificate, passport, citizenship certificate, visa
- Must prove Queensland residency (recent utility bill, rental agreement, etc.)

Step 3: Apply for licence
- Apply at TMR customer service centre or online (if available)
- Pay application fee: Approximately $40-50 (subject to change)
- Will receive Learner Driver Licence Receipt initially
- Physical licence card arrives in mail within 10-15 business days
- Can start driving immediately with receipt

Step 4: Receive logbook
- Automatic receipt of Queensland learner logbook with physical licence
- Records supervised driving hours
- Digital logbook alternative available through RACQ Learn2Go online

KNOWLEDGE TEST OVERVIEW
- Test based on "Your Keys to Driving in Queensland" handbook
- Written test or PrepL online program completion
- Tests knowledge of: road signs, road rules, safe driving practices
- Pass mark: Typically 80-90% (specific threshold set by TMR)
- Can attempt multiple times if unsuccessful
- PrepL program includes interactive learning modules and assessment

FEE STRUCTURE (As of July 2024 - subject to indexation)
- Learner licence application: ~$40-50
- Licence fees frozen for 12 months from July 2024
- Replacement licence card: ~$10-15
- Replacement logbook: ~$5
- Driver Knowledge Test (if doing written test instead of PrepL): ~$30-40

PRACTICAL TIPS FOR LEARNER APPLICATION
- Complete PrepL program for better understanding before driving (takes 2-4 hours)
- Prepare documentation beforehand to avoid delays
- Take receipt with you when first driving - counts as valid licence
- Get logbook started immediately - hours only count from licence issue date
- Join RACQ or similar motor club for logbook support and driver training advice

PART 2: LEARNER DRIVER RESTRICTIONS
================================================================================

L-PLATE DISPLAY REQUIREMENTS

Physical L-Plate Specifications:
- Colour: Yellow background with black uppercase letter "L"
- Minimum size: 14.6cm x 14.6cm (approximately A6 size)
- Lettering: Bold, clear, capital "L"
- Visibility: Must be clearly visible from 20 metres away
- Placement: One L-plate at front of vehicle, one at rear
- Motorcycles: L-plate at rear only
- Can purchase pre-made plates or print approved template

Penalties for Non-Display:
- Learner driver: 2 demerit points + $230 fine
- Supervising driver: 2 demerit points + $230 fine
- Both driver and supervisor can be fined
- Not displaying affects insurance validity

SUPERVISION REQUIREMENTS

Mandatory Supervising Driver:
- Must have valid open (unrestricted) licence for vehicle class being driven
- Must hold that licence for minimum 1 year continuously
- Must be seated in front passenger seat at all times
- Cannot be provisional (P) licence holder or learner driver
- Can be accredited driving instructor (bonus hours apply)

Penalty for Driving Without Supervisor:
- 4 demerit points + $230 fine

BLOOD ALCOHOL CONTENT (BAC) RESTRICTIONS

Learner Driver BAC Limit:
- 0.00% BAC - Absolutely zero tolerance
- Cannot have ANY alcohol in system
- Random breath testing applies
- Applies anywhere in Australia

Supervisor BAC Requirement:
- Must be under legal limit (0.05% BAC)
- Supervisors subject to random breath testing
- Same rules apply as general driving public

Penalties for Driving Over Limit:
- BAC 0.01-0.04%: From $297 fine + 4 demerit points
- BAC 0.05-0.07%: From $406 fine + 4 demerit points
- BAC 0.08%+: Court appearance, significant fines, licence suspension
- Higher penalties for repeat offences

SPEED LIMIT RESTRICTIONS

Learner Driver Speed Limit:
- Maximum 100 km/h for all conditions
- Must follow posted speed limits if lower than 100 km/h
- Applies even if vehicle capable of higher speeds
- No exceptions for motorways or dual carriageways

Speed Limit Compliance:
- Speeding 1-10 km/h over limit: 1 demerit point + fine
- Speeding 11-20 km/h over limit: 3 demerit points + fine
- Speeding 21-30 km/h over limit: 5 demerit points + fine
- Speeding 30+ km/h over limit: 8 demerit points + fine

School Zone Restrictions:
- School zones: 40 km/h during school hours
- Operating times: 8:00-9:30am, 2:30-4:00pm school days
- Must comply regardless of learner 100 km/h limit

TOWING RESTRICTIONS

What Learner Drivers Can Tow:
- Can tow trailer, caravan, or other vehicle (with restrictions)
- L-plates must be displayed on rear of towed item
- Limited to vehicles under specific weight limits
- Not recommended for new learners

Towing Recommendations:
- Most learners should avoid towing until experienced
- Check vehicle manual for towing specifications
- Supervisor should have towing experience
- Insurance must cover towing operation

VEHICLE TYPE RESTRICTIONS

High-Powered Vehicle (HPV) Restrictions:
Learner drivers CANNOT drive HPVs. Restricted vehicles include:
- Vehicles built after 1 Jan 2010 with power-to-weight ratio exceeding 130kW/tonne
- V8 engines
- Turbocharged or supercharged vehicles
- Modified high-performance vehicles
- Most common sports cars and performance sedans

Permitted Vehicle Types:
- Standard family vehicles (sedans, hatchbacks)
- SUVs/4WDs below HPV threshold
- Vans (within size restrictions)
- Utes below HPV threshold

PASSENGER RESTRICTIONS

Number of Passengers:
- NO specific limit on number of passengers
- Must ensure all passengers wear seatbelts
- Supervisor must be in front passenger seat
- Practical limit: Too many passengers can be distracting

Important Note on Under-25 Age Group:
- Passengers and supervisor cannot use mobile phone on loudspeaker
- Passengers cannot use video call function
- This applies if learner driver is under 25

Mobile Phone Use by Passengers:
- Supervisor/passengers using phone on loudspeaker: $1,251 fine (if learner under 25)
- Drivers caught can also be fined if permitting it

TIME RESTRICTIONS
- NO specific time restrictions for learner drivers
- Can drive anytime day or night
- School zones affect times (see school zone section)
- Night driving experience important for competency

LOCATION RESTRICTIONS
- NO geographic restrictions on where learner can drive
- Can drive on all public roads (including motorways)
- Can drive interstate (subject to that state's learner rules)
- Cannot drive on closed roads or private roads in certain circumstances

COMMON LEARNER DRIVER MISTAKES TO AVOID
1. Not displaying L-plates or displaying incorrectly (costs 2 demerit points)
2. Driving without supervisor present
3. Exceeding 100 km/h limit
4. Inadequate supervision - supervisor looking at phone/distracted
5. Not recording logbook hours accurately
6. Assuming passenger restrictions same as other states
7. Driving high-powered vehicles
8. Using mobile phone in any way
9. Not adjusting driving for learner 100 km/h limit on expressways
10. Not building sufficient night driving experience before test

PART 3: DEMERIT POINTS AND PENALTIES
================================================================================

DEMERIT POINT SYSTEM OVERVIEW

How the System Works:
- Learner drivers start with ZERO demerit points
- Points ACCUMULATE (not deducted) with each traffic offence
- Different offences attract different point values
- Points stay on traffic record for 3 years from date of offence

Accumulation Period:
- 12-month continuous period for assessment
- Points from offences committed outside Queensland still count
- Applies to driving anywhere in Australia

DEMERIT POINT THRESHOLD AND SUSPENSION

Learner Licence Threshold:
- 4 or more demerit points in any 12-month continuous period
- Automatic 3-month licence suspension
- No option for good driving behaviour period
- Mandatory suspension - no choices available

Suspension Procedures:
- Receive "Accumulation of Demerit Points Notice" in mail
- Suspension starts on date specified in notice
- Can request earlier start date if desired
- Do NOT need to surrender licence card
- Automatic reinstatement after 3 months
- Suspension time does NOT count toward minimum holding period

What Happens During Suspension:
- Cannot legally drive during suspension period
- Driving while suspended is criminal offence (not just traffic offence)
- Can face up to $4,400 fine and/or 1 year jail
- Licence automatically reinstated after 3 months (unless expired)

TOP 15 COMMON OFFENCES AND DEMERIT POINTS

Point Value 1:
- Speeding 1-10 km/h over limit
- Failing to carry licence
- Incorrect distance from vehicle ahead

Point Value 2:
- Not displaying L-plates correctly
- Failing to dim high-beam lights
- Improper use of parking lights

Point Value 3:
- Speeding 11-20 km/h over limit
- Failing to give way at intersection
- Not obeying traffic lights
- Failing to stop at stop sign
- Improper indicating/signalling
- Unsafe lane change
- Failure to wear seatbelt

Point Value 4:
- Using mobile phone held in hand
- Supervisor not wearing seatbelt
- Driving without required supervision
- Failing to remove L-plates (if licensed driver)
- Passenger not wearing seatbelt

Point Value 5:
- Speeding 21-30 km/h over limit
- Dangerous driving behavior
- Failing to give way to pedestrian
- Improper turning procedures

Point Value 6:
- Speeding 30+ km/h under limit

Point Value 8:
- Speeding 30+ km/h over limit
- Reckless driving

SAMPLE FINE AMOUNTS (As of July 2024 - subject to change)

Note: All figures are approximate and subject to annual indexation (4.2% in July 2024)

Mobile Phone Offence:
- Standard offence: $1,251 fine + 4 demerit points
- Repeat offence within 12 months: $1,251 + 8 demerit points (double)
- Caught on camera: $1,251 + 4 demerit points

Not Displaying L-Plates:
- Fine: $230 + 2 demerit points (each party)

Driving Without Supervision:
- Fine: $230 + 4 demerit points

Speeding Offences (approximate):
- 1-10 km/h over: $75-150 + 1 demerit
- 11-20 km/h over: $150-250 + 3 demerit
- 21-30 km/h over: $250-400 + 5 demerit
- 30+ km/h over: $400-600 + 8 demerit

Seatbelt Violation:
- Driver: $1,251 + 4 demerit points
- Passenger: $1,251 + 4 demerit points
- Repeat: Double demerit points in 12 months

School Zone Violation:
- Variable depending on speed infraction
- Typically $150-300 fine + 3 demerit points

Traffic Light/Stop Sign Violation:
- Fine: $200-300 + 3 demerit points

Not Giving Way:
- Fine: $200-300 + 3 demerit points

Drink Driving (0.01-0.04% BAC):
- Fine: $297 + 4 demerit points

SUSPENSION PROCEDURES

Initial Notice:
- Written notice sent by mail: "Accumulation of Demerit Points Notice"
- Clearly states suspension start date
- 28 days to object if wished (for extenuating circumstances)

Choosing Suspension Date:
- Suspension can start early if requested
- Must contact TMR to request early start
- Once started, cannot be delayed

During Suspension:
- Absolutely cannot drive
- License remains valid (don't need to surrender it)
- No driving for any purpose - fully illegal
- Insurance void if driving while suspended

After Suspension:
- Licence automatically reinstated
- Clean slate - points don't carry forward in same way
- Can continue driving with same restrictions
- Accumulation clock resets

DEMERIT POINTS RETENTION

How Long Points Stay on Record:
- Most demerit points: 3 years from date of offence
- High-speed offences: 5 years
- Serious offences (drink driving, etc.): Longer periods possible
- Points from driving outside QLD still apply

Checking Your Points:
- Online service: Check My Demerit Points (QLD Government website)
- Phone: 13 23 80 (TMR Customer Service)
- Points visible in Traffic History Report

DOUBLE DEMERIT POINTS

When Double Points Apply:
- Repeat offence in same category within 12 months
- Mobile phone offence repeat: 8 demerit points (instead of 4)
- Seatbelt offence repeat: 8 demerit points (instead of 4)
- Serious offences: Can accumulate up to 16 demerit points

Example: Double Points in Action
- First mobile phone offence: 4 demerit points
- Second mobile phone offence within 12 months: 8 demerit points (DOUBLE)
- Total accumulated: 12 demerit points (would trigger suspension)

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS FOR LEARNERS

No Good Driving Behaviour Period:
- Learners cannot choose 1-year behaviour period (available to P-plate drivers)
- Must accept 3-month suspension
- Suspension is mandatory, not optional

Suspension Doesn't Count Toward Holding Period:
- If suspended during learner phase, suspension time doesn't count
- Still must complete minimum holding periods AFTER suspension ends
- Example: Suspended after 10 months as learner, then 3-month suspension
  = must still hold full remaining period after suspension

Impact on Progression to P-Plates:
- Suspension doesn't prevent progression to P-plates
- Just delays timeline
- Must still complete other requirements (hours, logbook, testing)

PART 4: ROAD RULES FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================================

TRAFFIC LIGHTS AND SIGNALS

Red Traffic Light:
- Must stop completely before the stop line
- Do not proceed until light turns green
- Can turn left if safe (unless "No Left Turn on Red" sign)
- Turning left still means giving way to oncoming traffic/pedestrians

Yellow/Amber Traffic Light:
- Means stop - do NOT accelerate to run the light
- Only proceed through if unable to stop safely
- If already in intersection when light turns yellow, complete movement
- Accelerating toward yellow light is dangerous and unsafe

Green Traffic Light:
- Only proceed if intersection clear
- If already in intersection, complete turn on yellow/red if safe
- Must still give way to pedestrians and cross-traffic
- Green arrow: Can proceed in direction of arrow

Pedestrian Signals (Red/Green Figures):
- Red figure (stand): Do not cross - stop at kerb
- Green figure (walk): Safe to cross if no vehicles approaching
- Flashing green: Indicates pedestrian time running out
- Always confirm intersection clear before entering

ESSENTIAL TRAFFIC SIGNS

Regulatory Signs (Must Obey):
- STOP sign (octagon, red border): Complete stop, give way to all traffic/pedestrians
- GIVE WAY sign (triangle, red border): Slow down, stop if necessary, give way
- NO ENTRY sign (circle, red background): Do not enter - wrong way
- DO NOT PASS/NO OVERTAKE: Cannot overtake on this stretch
- Directional arrows: Indicating mandatory direction (red = prohibited direction)

Warning Signs (Alert Drivers):
- School zone signs: Warns of school - 40 km/h in designated hours
- Pedestrian crossing ahead: Pedestrians may be present
- Curve ahead: Road curves sharply - reduce speed
- Steep hill: Upcoming incline - prepare for hills
- Slippery road: Reduced grip - decrease speed, increase following distance
- Double lines ahead: No overtaking zone approaching

Information Signs (Provide Information):
- Speed limit signs (white circle with black number): Maximum legal speed
- Parking signs: Indicating parking restrictions/allowances
- Direction signs: Indicating direction to destinations
- One-way signs: Traffic flow direction on this road

ROAD MARKINGS AND LANE LINES

White Lines (Lane Dividers):
- Broken white line: Can cross to overtake or change lanes (if safe)
- Solid white line: Should not cross except in emergency
- Double solid white lines: Absolutely cannot cross (applies both directions)
- Edge white lines: Define road boundaries

Yellow Lines (Parking Restrictions):
- Single yellow line: No parking during restricted times (signs show times)
- Double yellow lines: No parking at any time
- Line on kerb: Parking restriction applies along that length

Lane Markings:
- Solid lane marking: Stay in lane, do not change
- Broken lane marking: Can change lanes if safe
- Arrows on road: Indicate mandatory direction for that lane
- Bike lane markings: Separate lane for cyclists

SPEED LIMITS - GENERAL

Default Speed Limits (Unless Otherwise Signed):
- Built-up areas (residential/business): 50 km/h
- Open roads/country areas: 100 km/h
- Motorways/expressways: 100-110 km/h depending on conditions

Speed Limit Signs:
- Black number in white circle: Legal maximum
- Electronic variable signs: Adjust to conditions (congestion, weather)
- Temporary signs: School zones, roadworks, events

SCHOOL ZONES

Operating Times:
- School days: 8:00am-9:30am (morning)
- School days: 2:30pm-4:00pm (afternoon)
- Speed limit: 40 km/h
- Non-school days: Standard speed limit applies
- Holidays: Standard speed limit applies

Penalties for School Zone Violations:
- Fine: $150-300 (varies by speed)
- Demerit points: 3 points typical
- Monitoring: School zone cameras active during operating times
- Common infraction - enforce strictly

Examples:
- Driving 50 km/h in 40 km/h school zone = 1-10 km/h over = 1 point + fine
- Driving 60 km/h in 40 km/h school zone = 11-20 km/h over = 3 points + fine

SAFE SPEED PRINCIPLES

What is a Safe Speed?
- Speed appropriate for current conditions
- Allows stopping within visible distance
- Accounts for road surface, weather, visibility
- Reduced for heavy traffic, pedestrian areas, wet roads
- Even if below speed limit, may still be unsafe to drive faster

Factors Affecting Safe Speed:
- Wet or slippery roads: Reduce speed 10-20%
- Fog or poor visibility: Reduce speed significantly
- Heavy traffic: Match flow, maintain safe distance
- Night driving: Headlight range determines safe speed
- Residential areas: Lower speeds protect pedestrians

STOPPING DISTANCES (CRITICAL KNOWLEDGE)

Stopping Distance = Reaction Distance + Braking Distance

At 60 km/h:
- Reaction distance (1 second): ~17 metres
- Braking distance: ~37 metres
- Total stopping distance: ~54 metres
- This assumes good conditions and alert driver

At 100 km/h:
- Reaction distance: ~28 metres
- Braking distance: ~63 metres
- Total stopping distance: ~91 metres
- Poor conditions can double this distance

Key Point for Learners:
- Double speed ≠ double stopping distance (actually increases significantly)
- 100 km/h requires approximately 4x the stopping distance of 25 km/h
- Always maintain adequate space between vehicles
- Night driving increases stopping distance (headlights only illuminate ~150m)

Practical Application:
- At 60 km/h: Keep minimum 2-second gap from vehicle ahead (roughly one car length per 5 km/h speed)
- At 100 km/h: Keep minimum 3-second gap from vehicle ahead
- Wet roads: Increase to 4-second gap minimum

PART 5: INTERSECTIONS AND GIVING WAY
================================================================================

UNCONTROLLED INTERSECTIONS (No Traffic Lights, Stop Signs, or Give Way Signs)

Basic Give Way Rule:
- Vehicle approaching from RIGHT has right of way
- If going straight: Give way to vehicle approaching from right
- If turning left: Give way to vehicle approaching from right AND oncoming traffic going straight or turning left
- If turning right: Give way to vehicle approaching from right AND oncoming traffic turning left
- If arriving simultaneously: Vehicle on RIGHT has right of way

Example Scenario - Straight Crossing Intersection:
- Your vehicle going west, vehicle approaching from east
- Vehicle from east has right of way
- You must give way even if you reached intersection first
- Safe procedure: Slow down, check clearly, proceed when safe

Example Scenario - Turning Left:
- You turning left into road, vehicle approaching from right going straight
- Must give way to that vehicle
- Also must give way to any oncoming traffic
- Wait until completely clear before commencing turn

T-INTERSECTIONS

Understanding T-Intersections:
- Main road continues straight (continuing road)
- Side road ends at main road (terminating road)
- Traffic on terminating road must yield to continuing road

From Terminating Road (Side Road):
- If turning left OR right onto continuing road: Give way to all traffic on continuing road
- Also give way to pedestrians crossing the main road
- Exception: Slip lane - can turn left using slip lane with different rules

From Continuing Road (Main Road):
- Generally have right of way
- Still must give way to pedestrians crossing
- Vehicles turning into side road still have priority

Penalties for Failing to Give Way at T-Intersection:
- Fine: $200-300
- Demerit points: 3 points
- Increased penalties if causing accident

UNCONTROLLED INTERSECTIONS (Cross-Road - Four-Way)

Right-Turn Rule:
- Vehicle on RIGHT has priority
- Approaching from right? You have right of way
- Approaching from left? Give way to vehicle from right
- Approaching head-on from opposite direction? Different rules apply

Turning at Uncontrolled Intersection:
- Turning left: Give way to all oncoming traffic AND vehicles from right
- Turning right: Give way to vehicle from right only
- Going straight: Give way to vehicle from right only
- If arriving simultaneously: Right-approaching vehicle goes first

Multiple Vehicles Arriving Simultaneously:
- Vehicle from right goes first
- If two vehicles from perpendicular roads arrive simultaneously = right rule applies
- Always err on side of caution - let other vehicle proceed

ROUNDABOUTS (Single Lane and Multi-Lane Basics)

Entering a Roundabout - Fundamental Rule:
- MUST give way to all traffic ALREADY on the roundabout
- All traffic on roundabout has priority
- Slow down as approaching, be prepared to stop
- Only proceed when completely clear
- Look to the right for traffic already circulating

Single-Lane Roundabout Navigation:
1. Indicate left (except going straight ahead)
2. Approach slowly
3. Give way to traffic on roundabout (coming from right)
4. Drive clockwise around roundabout
5. Indicate left when ready to exit
6. Exit at your required road

Multi-Lane Roundabout Navigation:
- Enter from correct lane based on intended exit
- Right turn: Outer/right lane on entry
- Straight: Middle lanes depending on layout
- Left turn: Left/inner lane on entry
- Give way to vehicles exiting adjacent lanes
- Maintain lane discipline throughout

Direction Indicating at Roundabouts:
- Entering going straight: NO indicator required
- Entering turning right: Indicate RIGHT
- Entering turning left: Indicate LEFT
- Exiting: ALWAYS indicate LEFT (even if went straight)
- Signal left just before exiting (not on entry if going straight)

Common Roundabout Mistakes:
- Not giving way to traffic on roundabout (most common cause of roundabout crashes)
- Wrong lane selection causing late lane changes
- Not indicating when exiting (hitting car exiting nearby)
- Stopping suddenly if vehicle exits in front
- Not maintaining speed through roundabout
- Assuming other drivers will follow rules

Penalties for Roundabout Violations:
- Not giving way: 3 demerit points + $200-300 fine
- Improper indicating: 3 demerit points + $200-300 fine
- Traveling wrong direction: 3 demerit points + $200-300 fine

GIVE WAY RULES AT DIFFERENT INTERSECTION TYPES

Traffic Light Intersection:
- Follow traffic light colours (red, yellow, green)
- Green light: Safe to proceed but check for turning traffic
- Yellow light: Do not enter unless cannot stop safely
- Red light: Complete stop - except can turn left if safe (check locally)

Stop Sign Intersection:
- Come to complete stop at stop line or intersection
- Give way to all traffic and pedestrians
- Only proceed when completely clear
- Apply 3-5 second count before proceeding

Give Way Sign Intersection:
- Slow down and prepare to stop
- Give way to all oncoming and cross traffic
- Do not stop if safe to proceed at reduced speed
- Yield to pedestrians and cyclists

PEDESTRIANS AT INTERSECTIONS (Critical Rule)

Key Rule - Always Apply:
- When turning at ANY intersection (traffic lights or not)
- Must give way to ALL pedestrians crossing the road you are entering
- Pedestrians have absolute priority
- Includes: People on foot, wheelchair users, mobility device users

Pedestrian Crossing Situations:
- Pedestrian on pedestrian crossing: Always give way
- Pedestrian crossing outside crossing: Still must give way
- Pedestrian about to enter crossing: Must give way
- Pedestrian finished crossing but still on road: Must give way

Examples - Turning at Intersection:
- Turning left, pedestrian crossing from right: Give way
- Turning right, pedestrian crossing from left: Give way
- Going straight, pedestrian crossing ahead: Give way
- These apply regardless of traffic light status

Penalties for Not Giving Way to Pedestrians:
- Fine: $200-300
- Demerit points: 3 points
- Increased to 5 points if causing injury
- Insurance may not cover if your fault

PART 6: TURNING AND MANEUVERING
================================================================================

LEFT TURNS

Standard Left Turn Procedure:
1. Check mirrors and blind spots (look over left shoulder)
2. Indicate left at least 50 metres before turn
3. Approach from left side of road (unless lane markers indicate differently)
4. Reduce speed appropriately
5. Proceed if safe - watch for oncoming traffic
6. Complete turn into appropriate lane of destination road
7. Stop indicating once turn is completed

Giving Way When Turning Left:
- Give way to oncoming traffic going straight
- Give way to oncoming traffic turning right
- Give way to any pedestrians crossing
- Give way to cyclists on crossing path
- Do NOT proceed until gap appears in traffic

Left Turn from Multi-Lane Road:
- Approach from the left lane
- Indicate left well in advance
- Ensure turn is possible without obstructing traffic
- Maintain lane position until exiting intersection

Left Turn Using Slip Lane:
- Slip lanes are left-turn lanes designed for continuous turning
- Different give way rules apply at slip lanes
- Still give way to pedestrians on or entering slip lane
- Can turn without waiting for major traffic gap (slip lanes designed for this)
- Maintain safe speed through slip lane

Turning Left at Traffic Lights:
- Green light: Can turn left if oncoming traffic clear
- Red arrow: Absolutely cannot turn left
- Yellow light: Complete turn if safe and started during green phase
- Give way to any pedestrians or cross-traffic

STRAIGHT-AHEAD AT INTERSECTIONS

Proceeding Straight - General Rules:
- At traffic lights: Proceed on green if intersection clear
- At uncontrolled intersection: Give way to traffic from right
- At give way sign: Slow down, ensure clear before proceeding
- Maintain lane discipline - don't drift across lanes

Confirming Intersection is Clear:
- Quick left-right-left check of intersection
- Ensure no vehicles turning across your path
- Check for pedestrians or cyclists
- Look in all directions for potential hazards

Speed Through Intersection:
- Maintain steady, controlled speed
- Don't accelerate in intersection
- Reduced speed if any doubt about safety
- Speed appropriate to road conditions

RIGHT TURNS

Standard Right Turn Procedure:
1. Check mirrors and blind spots (look over right shoulder)
2. Indicate right at least 50 metres before turn
3. Approach from right side of road (if safe and possible)
4. Reduce speed appropriately
5. Give way to oncoming traffic
6. Proceed when safe - watch for pedestrians
7. Complete turn into appropriate lane
8. Stop indicating once turn completed

Giving Way When Turning Right:
- Give way to oncoming traffic (very important)
- Oncoming traffic going straight has priority
- Oncoming traffic turning left has priority
- Must give way to pedestrians crossing destination road
- Do NOT proceed until gap appears in oncoming traffic

Right Turn from Multi-Lane Road:
- Position in right lane (or rightmost lane)
- Indicate well in advance (50+ metres)
- Check side mirrors for vehicles in right lane
- Wait for oncoming traffic to clear completely
- Turn into appropriate lane of destination road

Right Turn at Traffic Lights:
- Green light: Can turn right if oncoming traffic and pedestrians clear
- Red arrow: Cannot turn right
- Can turn right on red if no oncoming traffic (not at all intersections)
- Pedestrians have absolute priority

Protected vs. Unprotected Right Turns:
- Protected right turn (green right arrow): Oncoming traffic stopped, can proceed
- Unprotected right turn (green light): Must wait for oncoming gap - wait for other traffic to clear
- Green arrow is very common in Queensland cities

U-TURNS

Where U-Turns Permitted:
- Only where no "No U-Turn" sign present
- At traffic lights: Only if "U-Turn Permitted" sign displayed
- NOT at intersections with traffic lights unless specifically signed
- At uncontrolled intersections: Usually permitted (if clear view)

U-Turn Safety Requirements:
- Must have clear view of all approaching traffic
- Must not obstruct traffic flow
- Must be able to complete turn safely without stopping
- Give way to all vehicles and pedestrians
- Impossible in many busy locations due to traffic volume

Prohibited U-Turn Locations:
- Across single continuous line: Cannot cross
- Across double continuous lines: Cannot cross
- At intersections with "No U-Turn" signs: Prohibited
- In school zones (unless specifically permitted)
- At pedestrian crossings
- Where visibility restricted

U-Turn Penalties:
- Illegal U-turn: $200-300 fine + 3 demerit points
- Most common in city areas with heavy traffic

THREE-POINT TURNS (Three-Point Turn)

When Three-Point Turn Used:
- Reversing maneuver to change direction on narrow road
- Safer than attempting full U-turn on busy road
- Common in residential streets and narrow roads
- Takes more space and time than direct U-turn

Three-Point Turn Procedure:
1. Check mirrors and blind spots completely
2. Indicate right, ensure road is clear
3. Turn right to near side of road (45-degree angle approximately)
4. Stop and put in reverse
5. Turn wheels left full lock (or appropriate angle)
6. Reverse while turning to opposite side of road
7. Stop in reverse
8. Move forward (put in drive) turning right full lock
9. Proceed in opposite direction

Safety Considerations:
- Indicate clearly throughout maneuver
- Check mirrors constantly - especially when reversing
- Be aware of pedestrians and cyclists who may approach during turn
- Move slowly and deliberately
- Stop if any vehicle or pedestrian approaches
- Only attempt on quiet roads with good visibility
- Never perform on main roads or busy intersections

HOOK TURNS (Queensland Specific)

Hook Turn Definition:
- Left turn made by proceeding straight through intersection, moving to right side of road, stopping, then turning left
- Unusual maneuver - not common except at specific intersections
- Creates safety benefits for cyclists

Where Hook Turns Used:
- Specific intersections in Queensland (usually CBD areas)
- Always signed with "Hook Turn" sign on road
- Not permitted unless specifically signed

Hook Turn Procedure:
1. Approach intersection in right lane (or rightmost available lane)
2. Go straight through intersection (even though turning left)
3. Position vehicle on right side of destination road
4. Stop and wait for signals to change
5. Turn left when appropriate traffic lights show
6. Complete turn and proceed

Important Notes:
- Hook turns have specific traffic light sequences
- Only perform where signed/permitted
- Not available on most roads in Queensland
- Most learners will never encounter these
- If unsure - do NOT attempt, find alternative route

PART 7: SPEED MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

DEFAULT SPEED LIMITS IN QUEENSLAND

Open Road/Country Areas:
- Speed limit: 100 km/h (unless signed differently)
- Applies on rural highways and country roads
- Can be lower (80, 70, 60 km/h) if signed
- Learner maximum: Cannot exceed 100 km/h anyway

Suburban/Residential Areas:
- Speed limit: 50 km/h (standard for built-up areas)
- Applies in residential streets, shopping areas, business districts
- Very common in urban Queensland areas
- Many residential streets have 50 km/h limit

Motorways/Major Highways:
- Speed limit: 100-110 km/h (varies by road)
- Most motorways around Brisbane: 100-110 km/h
- Some sections reduced to 80 km/h
- Variable speed signs adjust for conditions
- Learner maximum: 100 km/h (so cannot drive at 110 km/h sections)

Electronic Variable Speed Signs:
- Adjust speed limit in real time
- Reduce during congestion (e.g., 80 km/h in heavy traffic)
- Adjust for weather (rain, fog)
- Must obey displayed speed - overrides posted sign

Speed Limit Signs:
- Black number on white circle: Maximum legal speed
- Must not exceed (except in emergency)
- In good conditions - speed may still be unsafe
- Always respect sign, but may need to reduce further

Learner Driver Speed Limit:
- Absolute maximum: 100 km/h
- Must also respect posted limit if lower
- Cannot exceed 100 km/h even on clear motorway with high limit
- Applies everywhere - no exceptions

SCHOOL ZONES

School Zone Definition:
- Designated area near school (typically 200-300 metres)
- Clearly marked with yellow "School Zone" signs
- Speed limit: 40 km/h during operating hours

Operating Hours:
- School days (Monday-Friday during school term)
- Morning: 8:00am to 9:30am
- Afternoon: 2:30pm to 4:00pm
- Not weekends or school holidays (standard speed limit applies)

School Zone Examples:
- Weekday 8:15am (term time): 40 km/h zone - Driving 50 km/h = 1-10 km/h over
- Saturday 8:15am: Standard speed limit applies (50 km/h in residential area)
- School holidays Tuesday 8:15am: Standard speed limit applies
- Weekday 4:15pm (term time): 40 km/h zone applies

Penalties for School Zone Violations:
- 1-10 km/h over 40 km/h limit: $150 fine + 1 demerit point
- 11-20 km/h over (50-60 km/h): $250 fine + 3 demerit points
- 20+ km/h over: $400-600 fine + 5+ demerit points
- School zone cameras active during operating hours
- Fines often higher due to enforcement priority

SAFE SPEED PRINCIPLES

What is a Safe Speed?
- Speed at which you can stop within visible distance
- Appropriate to road/traffic/weather conditions
- Even below speed limit may be unsafe if conditions poor
- Speed selected by driver - driver's responsibility

Factors Affecting Safe Speed:

Weather Conditions:
- Wet roads: Increase following distance, reduce speed 10-20%
- Rain: Visibility reduced, road grip reduced - lower speed
- Fog: Severe visibility reduction - significantly reduce speed
- Ice/frost: Extreme reduced grip - travel slowly or not at all
- Strong wind: Reduces vehicle control - cautious speed

Road Conditions:
- Gravel/unsealed roads: Reduce speed - less grip
- Pothole/damaged roads: Reduce speed - risk of damage/loss of control
- Loose gravel: Reduce speed - may lose traction

Traffic Density:
- Heavy traffic: Match flow of traffic (usually reduced from speed limit)
- Empty road: Still maintain speed appropriate to visibility/conditions
- Rush hour: Typically lower effective speeds due to congestion

Visibility:
- Good visibility: Can use higher speeds (up to limit)
- Restricted visibility (pedestrians, vehicles): Reduce speed
- Night driving: Headlights typically illuminate ~150 metres - limit speed to stopping distance within that
- Curves/hills: Reduce speed if cannot see around obstacle

Pedestrian Areas:
- High foot traffic areas: 30-40 km/h appropriate
- Shopping districts: 40-50 km/h maximum
- Parks/playgrounds adjacent: Reduced speed appropriate
- Residential streets: 40-50 km/h typical

Learner Driver Safe Speeds:
- Learners should drive below the limit when learning
- Practice at various speeds - don't always drive at limit
- Build experience at lower speeds first (30-50 km/h)
- Gradually progress to higher speeds (60-80 km/h)
- Only approach 100 km/h when experienced and comfortable

STOPPING DISTANCES (Critical Knowledge for Learners)

Two Components of Stopping Distance:
1. Reaction distance: Distance traveled while driver reacts (typically 1 second)
2. Braking distance: Distance traveled while vehicle decelerates to stop

Reaction Time Factors:
- Normal alert driver: 1 second (average)
- Tired driver: 1-2+ seconds (significantly impaired)
- Distracted driver: 2-3+ seconds or longer
- Young drivers: Often slower reaction time

Reaction Distance at Different Speeds:
- 30 km/h: Approximately 8 metres in 1 second
- 50 km/h: Approximately 14 metres in 1 second
- 60 km/h: Approximately 17 metres in 1 second
- 80 km/h: Approximately 22 metres in 1 second
- 100 km/h: Approximately 28 metres in 1 second

Braking Distance - Key Distances:

At 50 km/h (Good Conditions):
- Braking distance: Approximately 22 metres
- Total stopping distance: ~36 metres (reaction + braking)
- Equals about 2.5 car lengths

At 60 km/h (Good Conditions):
- Braking distance: Approximately 37 metres
- Total stopping distance: ~54 metres (reaction + braking)
- Equals about 3.5 car lengths

At 80 km/h (Good Conditions):
- Braking distance: Approximately 63 metres
- Total stopping distance: ~85 metres (reaction + braking)
- Equals about 5.5 car lengths

At 100 km/h (Good Conditions):
- Braking distance: Approximately 91 metres
- Total stopping distance: ~119 metres (reaction + braking)
- Equals about 7.5 car lengths

The 2-Second Rule (Practical Application):

Basic Rule:
- Maintain at least 2 seconds between your vehicle and vehicle ahead
- At 60 km/h: 2 seconds = approximately one car length per 5 km/h speed
- At 100 km/h: 2 seconds = approximately 56 metres

How to Apply 2-Second Rule:
1. Choose fixed point ahead (road mark, tree, sign)
2. Note when vehicle ahead passes that point
3. Count how long until your vehicle reaches that point
4. Count: "One thousand and one, one thousand and two"
5. Should reach approximately 2 full seconds
6. If less than 2 seconds: Drop back - increase gap

Night Driving Stopping Distance:
- Headlight range typically ~150 metres maximum
- Cannot stop beyond what headlights show
- Speed must allow stopping within visible range
- Typically means 60-70 km/h maximum safe speed at night
- Significantly lower than daytime driving on same road

Wet/Poor Conditions:
- Double the stopping distance approximately
- What takes 54 metres in good conditions takes ~108 metres in wet conditions
- Increase following distance to 3-4 seconds in poor conditions
- Adjust speed downward when conditions deteriorate

SPEED LIMIT COMPLIANCE FOR LEARNERS

Common Learner Speed Mistakes:
1. Not respecting 100 km/h maximum (especially on expressways with higher posted limits)
2. Exceeding limits in school zones (expensive penalties)
3. Driving too fast for conditions (rain, curve, pedestrian area)
4. Not realizing modern vehicles accelerate easily beyond learner limit

Speed Management Practices:
- Set cruise control at 90 km/h on expressways (leaves 10 km/h margin)
- Actively monitor speedometer in school zones
- Reduce speed in residential areas (50 km/h common, easy to exceed)
- Consider posted speed as maximum, not target
- Build safety margin by driving 5-10 km/h below limit when learning

PART 8: PARKING REGULATIONS
================================================================================

PROHIBITED PARKING LOCATIONS

Absolutely NO Parking Allowed:
- On footpaths/sidewalks (except where permitted sign)
- In front of fire hydrants (no stopping OR parking)
- In No Parking zones (marked by double yellow lines)
- Within 10 metres of intersection (unless signed differently)
- In disabled parking spaces (unless displaying valid permit)
- In bus lanes during operating hours
- On clearway (no stopping area)
- Across driveway entrance
- Double lines (across or on - completely prohibited)
- On blind corners or where visibility restricted
- On tram tracks or trolley lines
- In no-stopping zones

Do NOT Park Signs:
- Commonly seen as red circle with white diagonal line
- Cannot stop or park in this area at any time
- Absolute prohibition

No Parking Signs (With Times):
- May show specific times when parking prohibited
- Outside those times: Parking permitted
- Example: "No Parking 8am-6pm Monday-Friday"
- Other times: Parking is allowed

Time-Limited Parking:
- "2 Hour Parking" sign: Maximum 2 hours, then must move vehicle
- "4 Hour Parking" sign: Maximum 4 hours, then must move vehicle
- P-signs indicate time limit: "P4" = 4 hours maximum
- Must display valid parking meter ticket or card

Clearway Zones:
- Marked by red X on road or "Clearway" sign
- No parking during specified hours (usually 7-10am, 4-7pm weekdays)
- Limited parking allowed outside these hours (if not otherwise prohibited)
- Common on major roads in Brisbane and cities

Residents Parking:
- Marked for residents only with valid permit
- Other drivers cannot park (will be ticketed)
- Permits issued by local councils
- Common in inner-city residential areas

Loading Zones:
- Marked with blue lines or "Loading Zone" sign
- For loading/unloading goods only
- Cannot park vehicle if not actively loading
- Time limit typically 10-15 minutes
- Common near shops and businesses

PARKING METHODS

Parallel Parking:
- Parking alongside road or between two vehicles
- Must indicate before maneuver
- Check mirrors and blind spots
- Use at least 1.5x vehicle length of space
- Position vehicle parallel to kerb (approximately 30cm from edge)
- Practice essential skill for driving test

Angle Parking:
- Parking at 45-degree angle to kerb
- Common in car parks and some streets
- Easier than parallel parking
- Less space required
- Clear visibility important when exiting

Reverse Parking:
- Reversing into parking space
- Required in many car parks
- Need at least 1.5x vehicle length
- Indicate reverse intent - slow approach
- Use mirrors constantly
- Ensure pedestrians/cyclists not in path

Perpendicular Parking (Head-In Parking):
- Driving straight into perpendicular parking space
- Common in car parks (head-in or reverse option)
- Need sufficient width and depth
- Monitor kerbs to avoid collision

Kerb Alignment:
- Park parallel to kerb (approximately 30-50cm away)
- Do not mount kerb
- Keep safe distance from kerb
- Too far away looks like improper parking

NO STOPPING vs NO PARKING

Key Difference - Critical Distinction:

No Stopping Zone:
- Cannot stop, park, or wait in vehicle
- Cannot even briefly pull over
- Applies 24/7 (unless times specified)
- Marked with red X or "No Stopping" sign
- Absolute prohibition on stopping for any reason
- Exception: Stopping required by traffic light/give way sign, emergency

No Parking Zone:
- CAN stop briefly (e.g., pick up/drop off person)
- CANNOT leave vehicle parked unattended
- OK for active loading/unloading
- Can be temporary (times specified) or 24/7
- Marked with single yellow lines or "No Parking" signs
- Different from "No Stopping" - key distinction

Example Application:
- "No Stopping" zone: Cannot pull over to use ATM or phone - must keep moving
- "No Parking" zone: Can pull over to drop off passenger, but must stay with vehicle and not leave it

COMMON PARKING SIGN TYPES

"P" Signs (Time-Limited Parking):
- P2 = 2 hours maximum
- P4 = 4 hours maximum
- P120 = 120 minutes (2 hours) maximum
- Must pay parking fee if applicable
- Need to move vehicle after time expires

"Permit" Signs:
- Permit parking - specific permit holders only
- Must display valid permit
- Non-permit vehicles will be ticketed
- Common in residential areas

Disabled (Accessible) Parking:
- Blue sign with wheelchair symbol
- For vehicles displaying valid disability permit
- Must be closest available to destination
- Towing fee + fine if illegally parked
- Significant penalty for parking without permit

Reserved Parking:
- "Reserved for..." sign
- Specific person/business only
- Vehicle can be towed if parked without authorization

Meter Parking:
- Parking meter visible (older style)
- Must pay appropriate fee
- Display receipt or token in vehicle
- Common in older CBD areas

On-Street Parking:
- Parking directly on street (not in car park)
- Must follow all signage and lines
- Parallel parking typically required
- Check for parking restrictions

STREET PARKING BEST PRACTICES

Checking Parking Legality:
1. Look for No Stopping signs or double yellow lines = cannot park
2. Check for No Parking signs or single yellow line + times
3. Confirm parking time allowed (if timed zone)
4. Look for resident-only or permit signs
5. Confirm vehicle not in driveway or within 10m of corner

Parking Safely:
- Park parallel to kerb (30-50cm distance)
- Do not block driveways
- Do not park where visibility restricted
- Leave adequate room for other vehicles to exit
- Do not park on pedestrian crossings
- Do not park in disabled spaces without permit

When Parking in Car Parks:
- Display parking ticket on dashboard
- Note level and location (take photo if needed)
- Lock vehicle
- Take valuables
- Park in well-lit, populated area if possible
- Avoid dark/isolated corners

Parking Meter Compliance:
- Arrive early - parking fills quickly during busy times
- Pay appropriate amount for intended stay
- Note expiry time
- Return before expiry to avoid fine
- If running late - move vehicle to avoid ticket

PARKING VIOLATION PENALTIES

Illegal Parking Fines (Approximate):
- Parking in No Parking zone: $75-150
- Parking in disabled space (without permit): $400-600
- Parking in no-stopping zone: $150-250
- Parking in bus lane: $150-250
- Parking on footpath: $100-200
- Parking across driveway: $100-200
- Expired meter parking: $75-150
- All subject to change - check with local council

Penalty Notice:
- Fine issued immediately as penalty notice
- Can pay or contest within specified timeframe
- If not paid: Can escalate to court proceedings
- Unpaid fines collected through SPER (State Penalties Enforcement Registry)

Multiple Violations:
- Repeated parking violations can accumulate
- Multiple fines in short time shows pattern
- Courts may impose additional penalties
- Vehicle immobilization possible for serious violations

PARKING TEST REQUIREMENTS

Parking in Driving Test:
- Practical driving test includes parking component
- Must demonstrate safe parallel parking
- Must show correct car park parking technique
- Q-Safe test (Queensland practical test) includes parking

Parallel Parking Test Procedure:
- Vehicle must be within 30cm of kerb
- Not touching kerb
- Approximately parallel to kerb line
- Completed without hitting other vehicles
- Must use combination of forward and reverse

Car Park Parking Test:
- Drive into parking space without hitting obstacles
- Position vehicle within marked lines
- Use mirror checks and appropriate speed
- Demonstrate awareness of other vehicles/pedestrians

PART 9: VULNERABLE ROAD USERS

PEDESTRIAN CROSSING RULES

Pedestrian Crossing Types:

Standard Pedestrian Crossing:
- Marked with white parallel lines on road
- Zebra crossing appearance
- Has pedestrian signal lights (red man/green man figure)
- Must give way to pedestrians using this crossing

Traffic Light Pedestrian Crossing:
- Pedestrian signals integrated with traffic lights
- Red figure: Do not cross
- Green figure: Safe to cross (check for turning traffic)
- Pedestrians have priority when light indicates

School Crossing:
- Indicated by yellow "School Crossing" sign
- Flagman/woman may be present to manage traffic
- Must obey flagman's directions (overrides traffic lights)
- Give way to all pedestrians crossing at school crossing
- Typically near schools at peak times (8-9am, 2-4pm)

Pedestrian Refuge/Median Crossing:
- Crossing with traffic island in middle
- Pedestrians cross in two stages
- Vehicle must give way to pedestrians on both sides
- Island provides refuge point for pedestrians

Key Rule - Turning Vehicles at Crossings:
- When turning at intersection: MUST give way to all pedestrians
- Even if light is green for turning vehicle
- Even if pedestrian crossing is not at intersection
- Pedestrians on or entering pedestrian crossing have priority
- Applies to all turning situations (left or right turns)

Pedestrian Right-of-Way:
- Pedestrians on pedestrian crossing: Always have right of way
- Pedestrians crossing outside marked crossing: Still have some protection
- On footpath/kerb: Vehicles must not mount or obstruct

Penalties for Not Giving Way to Pedestrians:
- Fine: $200-300
- Demerit points: 3 points
- If injury caused: 5+ demerit points possible
- Insurance implications if at fault

CYCLIST MINIMUM PASSING DISTANCES

Minimum Passing Distance for Cyclists:
- Urban areas (50 km/h or less): 1 metre minimum distance
- Non-urban areas (above 50 km/h): 1.5 metres minimum distance
- Must maintain clearance when passing
- Insufficient distance = traffic violation

How to Safely Pass Cyclists:
1. Check mirrors for traffic behind
2. Indicate to change lanes (if safe)
3. Move completely into next lane to pass
4. Maintain 1-1.5 metre clearance depending on area
5. Complete pass and return to lane
6. Indicate lane change completion

Passing Cyclists - Key Points:
- Do not pull out if oncoming traffic
- Wait for safe gap to pass
- Do not pass if any doubt about space
- Slow down if cannot pass safely
- On narrow roads: May need to wait for clear road to pass
- Never try to squeeze through narrow gap

Cyclist Crossing at Intersections:
- Cyclists may use bicycle crossing at traffic lights
- White bicycle symbol marks special bicycle area
- Give way to cyclists exiting this area
- Cyclists have priority from cycle crossing area

Bicycle Storage Area (At Traffic Lights):
- White bicycle symbols painted between two lines
- Separate waiting area for cyclists
- Give way to any vehicle in this area
- If green light for cycle area: Give way to entering cycles

Penalties for Unsafe Passing of Cyclists:
- Fine: $200-300
- Demerit points: 3 points
- If causing injury/accident: Increased penalties
- Driving without due care and attention possible (more serious)

SHARING ROAD WITH MOTORCYCLISTS

Motorcycle Characteristics:
- Smaller vehicle, less visible in traffic
- Can accelerate quickly
- May filter through traffic lanes
- Less stable in emergency situations
- More vulnerable in collisions

Safe Driving Around Motorcycles:
- Check mirrors carefully for motorcycles (easy to miss)
- Give motorcycles full lane space
- Do not crowd motorcycles
- Allow full turning radius for motorcycles
- Be aware motorcycles may filter lanes in traffic

Motorcycle Filtering (Lane Filtering):
- Legal in Queensland between slow/stationary vehicles
- Motorcycles move between lanes when traffic stationary/slow
- Must share road safely with motorcyclists doing this
- Do not block lanes to prevent filtering
- Stay aware of motorcycles filtering near your vehicle

Visibility for Motorcyclists:
- Motorcycles less visible due to small size
- Use headlights at all times (good practice)
- Avoid blind spots (position vehicle where motorcycle can see)
- Check for motorcycles when changing lanes
- Assume motorcyclists cannot always see you

SCHOOL CROSSINGS

School Crossing Management:
- Marked with yellow "School Crossing" sign
- May have traffic control person (crossing supervisor)
- Crossing supervisor may display stop sign
- Traffic controller's hand signals override traffic lights
- Crossing supervisor directs pedestrians and traffic flow

Responsibilities When Approaching School Crossing:
- Slow down when approaching (prepare to stop)
- Watch for pedestrians and crossing supervisor
- Obey hand signals from supervisor (mandatory)
- Stop if supervisor holds stop sign
- Give way to all pedestrians crossing
- Do not proceed until crossing clear and supervisor allows

Operating Times:
- Typically 8:00-9:30am (morning)
- Typically 2:30-4:00pm (afternoon)
- School days only
- Not weekends or holidays

Penalties for School Crossing Violations:
- Not stopping when required: 3 demerit points + $200-300 fine
- Causing accident at school crossing: Serious charges possible

EMERGENCY VEHICLES

Emergency Vehicle Priority:
- Ambulances, fire trucks, police vehicles: Highest priority
- Indicated by flashing lights and/or sirens
- Must give way and pull over if safe
- Clear path as much as possible

What to Do When Emergency Vehicle Approaches:
- Check mirrors to identify emergency vehicle
- Indicate and slow down
- Move to side of road (pull over) if possible
- Stop if vehicle needs to pass
- Do not try to keep up with or follow emergency vehicle
- Resume normal driving when emergency vehicle passes

Emergency Vehicle in Opposite Lane:
- If emergency vehicle approaches in opposite lane
- You must still give way (move to side)
- Do not block their path through traffic
- Assume they may cross center line or boundaries

Penalties for Not Giving Way to Emergency Vehicles:
- Fine: $200-400
- Demerit points: 3-4 points
- If causing accident: Serious charges

COMMON VULNERABLE USER SCENARIOS

Scenario 1 - Pedestrian Crossing at Intersection:
- You: Turning right at traffic light (green light)
- Pedestrian: Crossing from left side of intersection
- Action: MUST give way to pedestrian
- Pedestrian has priority over turning traffic

Scenario 2 - Cyclist in Bike Lane:
- You: Turning right from bike lane area
- Cyclist: Traveling straight in bike lane
- Action: MUST give way to cyclist
- Cyclist in dedicated lane has priority

Scenario 3 - Motorcycle Filtering in Traffic:
- You: In traffic congestion, stationary vehicle
- Motorcycle: Filtering between lanes
- Action: Do not block or obstruct
- Allow motorcycle to safely pass through

Scenario 4 - School Crossing During Operating Times:
- You: Approaching school crossing
- Pedestrians: Waiting or crossing with supervisor
- Action: Prepare to stop and give way
- Obey supervisor's signals

Scenario 5 - Elderly Pedestrian at Crossing:
- You: Approaching turning left at traffic light
- Pedestrian: Slowly crossing in front of turn path
- Action: Stop and wait - pedestrian priority
- Do not assume they will clear in time

PART 10: SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

SEATBELT LAWS

Seatbelt Mandatory:
- ALL occupants must wear seatbelts at all times while moving
- Driver responsible for ensuring compliance
- Even short trips or parked with engine running

Seatbelt Rules by Seating Position:

Front Seat Occupants:
- Must wear seatbelt at all times
- Adult wearing seatbelt: Driver's responsibility OR occupant's responsibility
- In Queensland: Both driver and passenger responsible
- If passenger refusing: Cannot force compliance but driver may be liable

Back Seat Occupants:
- Must wear seatbelt if available
- Driver responsible for ensuring compliance (if applicable)
- Must not interfere with children's restraints
- Back seat seatbelts just as important as front

Seatbelt Positioning:
- Diagonal strap across chest and shoulder
- Lap belt across hips/lower abdomen
- Not around neck (danger of strangulation)
- Not under arm or behind back
- Properly positioned for effectiveness

Seatbelt Violation Penalties:
- Not wearing seatbelt (driver): $1,251 fine + 4 demerit points
- Not wearing seatbelt (passenger): $1,251 fine + 4 demerit points
- Driver responsible for minor passengers not wearing seatbelts
- Repeat violation within 12 months: Double demerit points (8 points)

CHILD RESTRAINT REQUIREMENTS

Child Restraint by Age/Size:

Under 12 Months (or Under 9kg):
- Rear-facing child seat required (infant capsule)
- Must be approved/certified restraint
- Front seat positioning: No - must be rear seat
- Passenger airbag off if rear-facing in front seat (if applicable)

12-36 Months (9-18kg):
- Forward-facing child seat required
- Must be approved/certified restraint
- Typically rear seat (safest position)
- Front seat permitted but less safe

3-7 Years (18-36kg):
- Forward-facing child seat OR booster seat
- Depends on size and development
- Typically up to 1.2 metres tall or 25kg
- Must use approved restraint

Over 7 Years (Over 25kg):
- Can use adult seatbelt if tall enough (usually 140cm+)
- Booster seat if shorter
- Must be properly positioned seatbelt
- Do not allow improper seatbelt positioning

Child Restraint Compliance:
- All restraints must be approved/certified
- Incorrect installation common problem
- Many parents install incorrectly
- Proper installation critical for effectiveness
- Can get installation checked at car seat clinics

Penalties for Child Restraint Violations:
- Not using required restraint: $1,251 fine + 4 demerit points per child
- Multiple children unrestrained: Multiple fines
- Repeat violations: Double demerit points within 12 months

MOBILE PHONE USAGE RESTRICTIONS

Learner Driver Mobile Phone Rules (Under 25):

Complete Ban:
- Cannot use mobile phone while driving IN ANY WAY
- Cannot hold phone in hand
- Cannot have phone rest on body (lap, leg, etc.)
- Cannot use hands-free kits
- Cannot use Bluetooth accessories
- Cannot use wireless headsets
- Cannot use phone loudspeaker
- Cannot use phone at all - ZERO tolerance

Even While Stationary:
- Cannot use phone when vehicle stopped but not parked
- Only usable when safely parked (not just stopped in traffic)
- Can only use parked when:
  - Paying for goods/services at drive-through
  - Accessing or leaving road/car park
  - Presenting digital licence to police

Passenger Phone Use (If Learner Driver Under 25):
- Supervisor/passengers cannot use phone on loudspeaker
- Passengers cannot use video call function
- Regular (non-loudspeaker) phone use OK for passengers
- Violation: $1,251 fine for passenger or supervisor

Learner Driver Over 25:
- Similar restrictions for learner drivers 25+
- Cannot use handheld phone (same as general rule)
- CAN use hands-free in some circumstances
- Still cannot use loudspeaker when driving
- Better than under-25 rules but still restricted

Penalties for Mobile Phone Violations:
- First offence: $1,251 fine + 4 demerit points
- Repeat offence within 12 months: $1,251 fine + 8 demerit points (DOUBLE)
- Caught on camera: $1,251 fine + 4 demerit points
- Multiple camera enforcement of this offence

ESSENTIAL VEHICLE SAFETY REQUIREMENTS

Roadworthiness Standards:

Tyres:
- Minimum tread depth: 1.5mm across all areas
- Cannot have visible cords
- Must be properly inflated
- Check tyre pressure monthly
- Poor tyres = reduced braking effectiveness

Brakes:
- Must be fully functional
- Check fluid level
- Unusual noises = get checked
- Reduced braking = dangerous and illegal

Windscreen:
- Must be clear (no cracks in driver's vision area)
- Wipers must function
- Washer fluid topped up
- Defrost function working
- Windscreen damage can affect insurance

Lights:
- All lights must work (headlights, tail lights, brake lights)
- Indicate lights functioning
- Hazard lights operational
- Check before night driving
- Broken light = defect notice

Seatbelts:
- All seatbelts must function smoothly
- Check for tears or damage
- Ensure secure fastening
- Test before trip

Steering/Suspension:
- Steering must be responsive and smooth
- Suspension should not clunk or bang
- Steering wheel play should be minimal
- Get checked if abnormal noises

Horn:
- Must be functional
- Can be used to alert other drivers
- Not for aggression - only safety

Vehicle Registration:
- Current registration plate displayed
- Sticker current and visible
- Registration up to date
- Cannot drive with expired registration

Vehicle Inspection (Periodic):
- Light vehicle inspection every 2-5 years depending on age
- Safety and emissions checked
- Defect notice issued if fails
- Must rectify and re-test within timeframe

PRACTICAL VEHICLE SAFETY CHECKS

Pre-Drive Safety Checklist:
- Walk around vehicle - observe obvious damage
- Check tyres for appropriate inflation and tread
- Test lights (headlights, brake lights, indicators)
- Confirm mirrors adjusted
- Check seatbelts functional
- Confirm windscreen clear and wipers work
- Test brakes before leaving driveway
- Confirm all doors close securely

During Driving:
- Monitor dashboard warning lights
- Listen for unusual noises
- Feel for unusual vibrations
- Watch brake responsiveness
- Monitor steering responsiveness
- Check brakes feel normal throughout drive

Post-Drive Inspection:
- Listen for unusual noises while stopping
- Walk around vehicle checking for damage
- Confirm all lights turned off
- Ensure vehicle parked safely

Common Safety Issues to Report:
- Warning light on dashboard: Get scanned at mechanic
- Soft brakes or spongy feel: Have brakes checked immediately
- Steering feels loose: Suspension/steering issue - get checked
- Unusual noises or smells: Get vehicle inspected
- Tyre bulges or damage: Replace immediately

PART 11: IMPAIRMENT AND DISTRACTION
================================================================================

ALCOHOL LAWS FOR LEARNERS

Zero Tolerance Policy:
- Learner drivers: 0.00% BAC - absolutely no alcohol
- No exceptions - even trace amounts
- Applies everywhere in Australia
- Random breath testing performed

Why Zero Tolerance for Learners:
- Young/inexperienced drivers more affected by small alcohol amounts
- Less driving experience = more impairment with same BAC
- Slows reaction time significantly
- Increases risky decision-making
- Causes overconfidence in ability

Testing Procedures:
- Random breath testing conducted by police
- Roadside testing: Breath analyzer device
- Positive result: Preliminary test confirmed with more precise equipment
- Refusal to test: Serious charges

Penalties for Exceeding 0.00% BAC:

Very Low (0.01-0.04%):
- Fine: ~$297
- Demerit points: 4
- Licence suspension not automatic at this level

Low (0.05-0.07%):
- Fine: ~$406
- Demerit points: 4
- Still below general limit (0.05%) but learner has zero limit

0.08% or Higher:
- Court appearance: Mandatory
- Significant fine: $3,000-5,000+
- Demerit points: 4-7 (depending on level and circumstances)
- Licence suspension: 3-12 months
- Possible jail time: Up to 3 months for first offence
- Criminal record

Recidivist Penalties (Repeat Offenders):
- Second offence: Enhanced penalties
- Third offence: Severe penalties including jail time
- Mandatory alcohol education programs
- Medical evaluation required
- Ignition interlock device possible (12 months)

DRUG RESTRICTIONS

Drug Driving Laws:
- Cannot drive under influence of any drug (prescription or illegal)
- Includes cannabis, cocaine, amphetamine, ecstasy, heroin, etc.
- Some prescription medications can impair (check with pharmacist)
- Drug driving carries same penalties as alcohol

Testing for Drugs:
- Oral/saliva drug testing at roadside
- Blood test for confirmation if positive
- Tests for: THC, cocaine, amphetamine, ecstasy, heroin, opioids
- Can refuse test but serious consequences

Prescription Medications:
- Check if medication impairs driving ability
- Pharmacist can advise
- Some medications cause drowsiness, dizziness
- Do not drive if impaired by medication
- Even legal prescription meds can cause impairment

Penalties for Drug Driving:
- Same as alcohol drink-driving penalties
- Court appearance required (usually)
- $3,000-5,000+ fine
- Licence suspension: 6-12 months
- Jail time possible
- Criminal record
- Enhanced penalties for repeat offences

RANDOM TESTING PROCEDURES

Random Breath Testing (RBT):

Process:
- Police conduct roadside stop
- Request breath sample using breathalyzer device
- Preliminary test result provided
- If negative: Can proceed
- If positive: Taken for second test/blood sample

Driver's Rights:
- Can request witness present (not always practical)
- Can request copy of results
- Can challenge result if equipment faulty
- Refusal carries serious consequences

Consequences of Refusal:
- Fine: Up to $1,000
- Demerit points: May apply
- Deemed as serious offence (similar to high BAC)
- Court appearance likely
- Criminal record

Random Drug Testing:
- Oral/saliva tests performed
- Similar process to breath testing
- Confirmation by blood test if positive
- Same refusal consequences as RBT

FATIGUE MANAGEMENT BASICS

Learner Driver Fatigue Considerations:

Causes of Fatigue:
- Lack of sleep (less than 7-8 hours)
- Long drive sessions without break
- Monotonous driving (highway vs. varied roads)
- Driving late at night
- Early morning driving
- Driving after physical exertion
- Medication side effects

Effects of Fatigue on Driving:
- Slower reaction times (similar to alcohol impairment)
- Reduced concentration
- Impaired judgment
- Increased risk-taking
- Microsleeps (brief moments of sleep while driving)
- Inability to maintain lane position
- Reduced vigilance for hazards

Fatigue Indicators (Know These):
- Frequent yawning
- Heavy eyelids
- Difficulty focusing
- Nodding head
- Lane drifting
- Missing exits or getting lost
- Unable to remember driving last few minutes

Fatigue Management Strategies:

Before Driving:
- Get adequate sleep (7-8 hours minimum)
- Avoid long driving sessions
- Have light snack/coffee if tired
- Discuss route with supervisor

During Long Drives:
- Stop every 2 hours minimum
- Switch drivers if possible
- Short walk breaks
- Caffeine (coffee/energy drink) can help
- Open window/turn up music (temporary fixes only)
- Never fight fatigue - stop driving

Night Driving and Fatigue:
- Learners more fatigued at night
- Night driving slower and more concentration-intensive
- Build up hours gradually for night driving
- Do shorter night drives than day drives
- Avoid driving late at night when tired

Signs to Stop Driving:
- Cannot keep eyes open
- Cannot focus on road
- Multiple close calls
- Having trouble staying in lane
- Supervisor concerned about driving
- More than 2 yawns in 5 minutes

DISTRACTION MANAGEMENT

Learner Driver Distractions:

Main Distractions:
- Mobile phone (most serious for learners)
- Passengers (conversation, laughing, loud conversation)
- Eating/drinking
- Adjusting controls (radio, climate, seat)
- Looking at scenery/distractions outside car
- Dashboard displays/navigation screens
- Emotional state (anger, excitement, sadness)

Mobile Phone Distraction:
- Main cause of distraction crashes for young drivers
- Complete ban for learners removes this risk
- Hands-free still distracting even if not handheld
- Full attention required while driving

Passenger Distraction:
- Can be worse than solo driving
- Reduce conversation while concentrating on difficult driving
- Friends/family may not understand distraction risk
- Supervisor should help manage passenger activity

Eating/Drinking While Driving:
- Not illegal but not recommended
- Reduces attention/control
- Spill risk (distraction)
- Do this during breaks, not while driving
- Stay hydrated but pre-plan

Control Adjustments:
- Adjust radio, climate, seat BEFORE driving
- If adjustment needed: Pull over safely
- Do not adjust while driving or at traffic lights
- Set navigation/GPS before starting journey

Learner Driver Distraction Minimization:
- Focus entirely on driving task
- Minimize conversation early in learning
- Keep radio volume low
- Avoid eating/drinking
- Do not adjust controls while driving
- Tell passengers: "This is learning time - minimal talking please"
- Practice staying focused

IMPAIRMENT AND DISTRACTION TESTING

Testing by Police:

Field Sobriety Tests:
- Walk-the-line test
- One-leg stand test
- Horizontal eye movement test
- These assess coordination/balance
- Not definitive but indicate possible impairment
- Can be performed before breath test

Roadside Breath Testing:
- Preliminary breath analyzer
- Results may not be fully accurate
- Positive result confirmed with second test/blood sample

Drug Testing:
- Oral/saliva device at roadside
- Rapid results
- Blood test for confirmation if positive

Workplace/School Testing:
- Not applicable to learner drivers on public roads
- Different rules if tested at school/work

Challenging Test Results:
- Equipment must be calibrated correctly
- Test must be administered properly
- Can request independent testing
- Lawyer can help challenge results
- Many tests have been challenged successfully in court

PART 12: LOGBOOK AND PROGRESSION
================================================================================

LOGBOOK REQUIREMENTS

Purpose of Logbook:
- Records supervised driving hours
- Documents learning progress
- Required for progression to provisional licence
- Ensures safe skill development
- Helps identify weak areas needing practice

Logbook Availability:

Physical Logbook:
- Automatically provided with learner licence
- Paper-based record
- Must be carried when driving
- Signed by supervisor after each session

Digital Logbook:
- Online version available (RACQ Learn2Go)
- Alternative to physical logbook
- Same hours recorded
- Printable for evidence of hours
- Some prefer digital for convenience

Where to Get Logbook:
- Automatically provided with learner licence
- If lost: Can replace at TMR customer service centre (~$5)
- Digital version online through RACQ website

MINIMUM HOURS REQUIRED

Requirement Duration:

Under 25 Years Old:
- Minimum 100 hours supervised driving
- Minimum 10 hours of these must be night driving
- Night driving = between sunset and sunrise
- MUST hold learner licence for 12 months minimum
- Hours must be completed before taking practical test

25 Years Old or Older:
- NO minimum 100-hour logbook requirement
- Still encouraged to get plenty of driving experience
- Must hold learner licence for minimum 12 months
- Must pass Hazard Perception Test (after 6 months holding)

Hour Breakdown - Recommended Distribution:

Under 25 Distribution:
- Daytime hours: Approximately 90 hours
- Night-time hours: 10 hours minimum
- Should include: Various conditions, traffic types, road types
- Spread across: Multiple weeks/months for skill development

Practical Allocation (Suggested):
- Residential/suburban driving: 30 hours
- Urban/CBD driving: 25 hours
- Major roads/expressways: 20 hours
- Night driving: 10 hours
- Various weather/conditions: 15 hours

Important: Hours Only Count Under Certain Conditions:
- Logged in official logbook
- Supervised by approved supervisor
- Supervisor with valid open licence for 12+ months
- In vehicle driven by learner (not student driving in car with instructor only)
- After learner licence issued (hours before don't count)

PROFESSIONAL DRIVING LESSON BONUS HOURS

Accredited Driving Trainer:
- Professional instructor with accreditation
- Hours with instructor count as logged hours
- Particular benefit: Can substitute for some logbook hours
- Recommended for learning challenging skills

Professional Lesson Hour Conversion:
- Generally: 1 hour professional lesson = 1 hour logged
- Some states offer multiplier (not typical for QLD)
- QLD typically: 1:1 conversion ratio
- Instructor must sign logbook confirming hours

Benefits of Professional Lessons:
- Structured learning of specific skills
- Feedback on technique
- Introduction to more challenging driving situations
- Can accelerate learning in weak areas
- Professional perspective on capabilities
- Cost consideration: Professional lessons expensive but effective

Selecting Professional Instructor:
- Check accreditation/qualifications
- Get recommendations
- Discuss specific learning goals
- Confirm instructor knowledgeable about learner rules
- Check instructor reviews if available

SAFER DRIVERS COURSE (If Applicable)

Queensland Specific Information:
- No mandatory Safer Drivers Course requirement for learners
- Different from some other states (e.g., VIC)
- Not a requirement to progress to P-plates
- Optional but recommended by some driving schools

If Taking Optional Course:
- Would typically count as driving instruction hours
- Covers defensive driving techniques
- Includes hazard perception training
- Professional delivery of safe driving principles
- May not provide hour multiplier in QLD but good training

Other Safety Courses:
- Various private driving schools offer crash avoidance courses
- Can supplement logbook hours
- Not mandatory but recommended for safer driving

MINIMUM LEARNER PERIOD DURATION

Mandatory 12-Month Holding Period:
- Must hold learner licence for minimum 12 months
- Applies to all learners regardless of age
- Suspension time does NOT count toward this period
- Cannot progress to provisional/P-plates before 12 months

Time Calculation:
- Counts from date of learner licence issue
- Not from first day of driving
- Suspension creates gap but doesn't extend requirement
- If suspended at month 10 for 3 months: Must wait until month 13 minimum

Purpose of 12-Month Period:
- Allows skill development across seasons/weather
- Provides experience in varying conditions
- Develops safe habits
- Ensures sufficient supervised driving time
- Separates learner and provisional phases

REQUIREMENTS TO PROGRESS TO PROVISIONAL LICENCE

To Apply for P1 (Provisional) Licence - Age Under 25:

Must Have:
- Held learner licence for minimum 12 months
- Completed 100 hours supervised driving (10 hours night minimum)
- Recorded hours in official logbook
- Clean traffic history (no major violations)
- Pass Hazard Perception Test (after holding learner for 6+ months)
- Pass Q-Safe practical driving test
- Be at least 17 years old

Hazard Perception Test (HPT):

Purpose:
- Tests ability to recognize and respond to driving hazards
- Video-based test (not practical driving)
- Shows various driving scenarios
- Must identify hazards and click when seen
- No physical driving component

When Can Take:
- After holding learner licence for 6 months minimum
- Can take before 12-month period complete
- Can pass test multiple times
- Current pass lasts indefinitely (for next requirement)

Test Format:
- Online video-based test
- Multiple scenarios shown
- Driver identifies hazards by clicking
- Hazards include: Other vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists, road conditions, etc.
- Timed test (approximately 1 hour)

Passing Score:
- Specific pass mark set by TMR
- Approximately 50% correct typically required
- Can retake if unsuccessful
- No pass/fail consequence - just progress blocker

Q-Safe Practical Driving Test:

Purpose:
- Tests practical driving ability
- Assesses safe driving skills
- Checks ability to handle various driving situations
- Evaluates control and decision-making

What's Tested:
- Start/stop procedure
- Lane discipline
- Intersection navigation
- Turning procedures
- Parking (parallel and car park)
- Following distance
- Speed management
- Hazard recognition
- Safe decision-making

Test Duration:
- Approximately 45-60 minutes
- Mix of urban and open road driving
- Examiner provides directions
- Examiner assessment of safe driving

Pass Criteria:
- No major errors (immediately fails)
- Minimal minor errors
- Safe vehicle control throughout
- Appropriate speed and decision-making
- Hazard awareness demonstrated

Test Cost:
- Approximately $80-120 (subject to change)
- Paid at time of booking
- Can retake if unsuccessful (additional fee)

Preparing for Q-Safe Test:
- Practice parallel parking extensively
- Drive in various conditions and traffic situations
- Practice all intersection types
- Ensure vehicle in good working order
- Get supervisor feedback on driving
- Practice under test-like conditions
- Know route characteristics before test day
- Adequate sleep night before test

To Apply for P1 Licence - Age 25 and Over:

Must Have:
- Held learner licence for minimum 12 months
- Pass Hazard Perception Test (can take after 6 months holding)
- Pass Q-Safe practical driving test
- NO 100-hour logbook requirement
- Clean traffic history (no major violations)
- Be at least 17 years old

Note: 25+ year-olds exempt from 100-hour logbook but still
must hold learner licence for 12 months minimum.

PRACTICAL DRIVING TEST OVERVIEW

Q-Safe Test Breakdown:

Pre-Test Vehicle Checks:
- Demonstrate knowledge of vehicle safety (tyres, lights, etc.)
- Show you can adjust mirrors/seats
- Confirm understanding of vehicle controls
- Identify basic safety features

Starting and Stopping:
- Safe start procedure
- Proper use of brake/accelerator
- Smooth acceleration
- Controlled stopping
- Appropriate parking/stopping procedure

Lane Discipline:
- Maintaining correct lane
- Safe lane changes (where necessary)
- Proper indicating for lane changes
- Not drifting between lanes

Intersection Navigation:
- Approaching intersections at appropriate speed
- Recognizing traffic control (lights, signs, lines)
- Safe turning from intersections
- Appropriate giving way
- Safe pedestrian interaction

Turning Procedures:
- Proper indicating (50+ metres before)
- Appropriate speed for turn
- Safe vehicle control during turn
- Completing turn into correct lane
- Left and right turns assessed

Parking Demonstration:
- Parallel parking (front and rear)
- Three-point turn or bay parking
- Safe use of mirrors
- Appropriate use of brakes
- Control and precision

Hazard Recognition:
- Responding to pedestrians
- Recognizing and avoiding hazards
- Safe following distance
- Speed appropriate to hazards
- Decision-making quality

Examiners Assess:
- Vehicle control
- Mirror/head checks
- Hazard awareness
- Decision-making
- Speed selection
- Safety consciousness
- Ability to follow instructions

Major Test Failures (Automatic Fail):
- Colliding with object/vehicle
- Hitting pedestrian or cyclist
- Driving on wrong side of road
- Failing to stop at stop sign/red light
- Unsafe lane change causing hazard
- Examiner must take control for safety reasons

Minor Errors (Acceptable - May Show):
- Slightly imperfect parallel parking (still within lines)
- Minor hesitation at intersection (if resolved safely)
- Slight speed variance
- Unnecessary gear changes
- Minor mirror check gaps (if safe overall)

Test Day Tips:
- Arrive early - reduces stress
- Ensure vehicle in excellent condition
- Get good sleep night before
- Eat proper breakfast
- Avoid heavy meals before test
- Follow examiner instructions precisely
- Ask for clarification if unsure
- Drive to best of ability - not too cautious or reckless
- Remember examiner wants you to pass (not trying to fail you)

Pass/Fail Results:
- Given immediately after test
- Verbal feedback provided
- Written report if failed (explaining why)
- Retest available if failed
- Can book retest immediately
- Cost for retest (same as initial test)
- No waiting period between attempts

Common Test Failures:
- Inadequate mirror checks
- Unsafe lane changes
- Failure to give way at roundabout
- Incomplete parallel parking
- Hesitation/uncertainty at intersections
- Speed management issues
- Poor hazard recognition

Progression After P1 Licence:

P1 Licence Validity: Minimum 12 months
- Can drive with fewer restrictions than learner
- Still subject to young driver restrictions
- Mobile phone: Different rules for P1 under 25
- Night driving: Restriction for under 25 (between 11pm-5am)

To Progress to P2 Licence:
- Hold P1 licence for minimum 12 months
- Clean traffic history during P1 period
- Reapply for licence upgrade
- No additional testing required
- Straight progression if requirements met

To Progress to Open Licence:
- Hold P2 licence for minimum 24 months
- Clean traffic history during P2 period
- Reapply for licence upgrade
- No additional testing required
- Full driving privileges once granted

APPENDIX A: QUICK REFERENCE - TOP 20 COMMON LEARNER Q&A
================================================================================

Q1: Can I drive at 110 km/h on motorway sections posted at 110?
A: No. Learner maximum is 100 km/h regardless of posted limit. Plan extra time.

Q2: What happens if I get 4 demerit points?
A: Automatic 3-month licence suspension. Cannot drive during suspension.

Q3: Can my girlfriend be my supervisor?
A: Yes, if she holds valid open licence held 12+ months, in front passenger seat.

Q4: Do I need to display L-plates inside the vehicle?
A: No. L-plates external markers. Failure = 2 demerit points + $230 fine.

Q5: Can I use GPS on my phone?
A: If phone is secured and route set BEFORE driving. Cannot touch while driving.

Q6: What if I exceed 100 km/h accidentally?
A: Exceeding limit (even 1 km/h over) = demerit points + fines. Watch speedometer.

Q7: How many hours of night driving do I need?
A: Minimum 10 hours night driving (sunset to sunrise) out of 100-hour requirement.

Q8: Can a learner supervise another learner?
A: No. Supervisor must hold valid open licence 12+ months. Fully licensed only.

Q9: What happens if supervisor has 0.06% BAC?
A: Supervisor can be fined (not learner directly). Supervisor must be under 0.05%.

Q10: Do school zone hours count outside school hours?
A: Standard speed limit applies outside 8-9:30am and 2:30-4pm school day times.

APPENDIX B: LEARNER DRIVER MISTAKES TO AVOID
================================================================================

Top 10 Mistakes:
1. Exceeding 100 km/h on expressways with higher limits
2. Not displaying L-plates or displaying incorrectly
3. Driving in school zones at incorrect speeds
4. Using mobile phone any way whatsoever
5. Driving without proper supervisor
6. Not giving way at roundabouts
7. Poor mirror checks before lane changes
8. Inadequate following distances
9. Not recording logbook hours accurately
10. Failing to indicate 50+ metres before turning

Each mistake carries consequences ranging from fines and demerit points to
licence suspension. Learner drivers should prioritize safety over speed and
familiarize themselves with all Queensland road rules thoroughly before driving.
