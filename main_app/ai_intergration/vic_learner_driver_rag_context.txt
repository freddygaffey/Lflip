PART 1: LICENSING PROCEDURES
================================================================================

MINIMUM AGE AND ELIGIBILITY
---------------------------
Age: Minimum 16 years old to obtain learner permit
Enrollment: Can enroll in online test at 15 years 11 months (permit issued at 16)
Progression: Must be 18 years old to apply for probationary licence

OBTAINING LEARNER PERMIT - STEP BY STEP
----------------------------------------
1. Study "Road to Solo Driving" handbook (free PDF online or purchase physical copy)
2. Choose test method: Online (4-6 hour interactive course) or In-person at VicRoads
3. Online test: First attempt FREE as of 2022, available in English only
4. In-person: Computer-based, interpreter services available (free), appointment required
5. Pass knowledge test (covers road rules, signs, safe driving from handbook)
6. Pay fees: Learner permit ~$30-40, test fees vary, appointment fee $21.50 if in-person
7. Provide proof of identity (original documents: birth certificate, passport, Medicare card, etc.)
8. Receive temporary receipt (can practice driving immediately)
9. Permanent card mailed within one week to registered address

FEES (Approximate, Subject to Change)
--------------------------------------
Learner permit application: $30-40
Online knowledge test (first attempt): FREE
In-person knowledge test: ~$30
Appointment fee (in-person): $21.50
Physical handbook: $5-15
Check vicroads.vic.gov.au for current fees

KNOWLEDGE TEST CONTENT
----------------------
Test covers: Speed limits, give way rules, traffic signals, road signs, lane markings, safe following distances, hazard perception basics, driver responsibilities, learner restrictions, alcohol/drug laws, licensing system
Format: Multiple choice, computer-based, pass mark typically 27-28 out of 32 correct
Can retake if failed (additional fees apply)

PART 2: LEARNER DRIVER RESTRICTIONS
================================================================================

L-PLATE DISPLAY
---------------
MUST display L plates on front AND rear of vehicle at all times
Plates must be visible from 20 meters away
Yellow L on black background (standard Victorian L plates)
Can be placed on windscreens or external parts if windscreen placement not practical
Penalties: Fines and demerit points for non-display
Available at VicRoads centers, newsagencies, automotive stores

SUPERVISION REQUIREMENTS
------------------------
MUST have supervising driver in front passenger seat AT ALL TIMES
Cannot drive alone under any circumstances
Supervisor must hold:
- Current FULL (non-probationary) Victorian car driver licence, OR  
- Interstate or overseas driver licence held for 2+ years for vehicle category
Supervisor must carry licence and be alert
Supervisor BAC limit: 0.05 (professional instructors: 0.00)
Supervisor cannot supervise multiple learners simultaneously
Must complete details in learner's logbook and sign off hours
Both learner and supervisor penalized if unqualified supervision

ALCOHOL AND DRUGS
-----------------
ZERO BAC (0.00) required at ALL times - no exceptions
No alcohol consumption permitted before or during driving
Subject to random breath and drug testing
Zero tolerance for illicit drugs
Prescription medication must not impair driving
Penalties: Automatic permit suspension, heavy fines, court appearance, demerit points, may affect progression to probationary licence

SPEED RESTRICTIONS
------------------
NO specific speed limit for Victorian learners (unlike NSW's 90 km/h limit)
Must obey ALL posted speed limits
Must drive at safe speed for conditions
Supervising driver must ensure safe speeds
Default limits: 50 km/h built-up areas, 100 km/h open roads, 40 km/h school zones

TOWING RESTRICTIONS
-------------------
CANNOT tow trailers or any other vehicle - absolute ban
No exceptions (except tractor in specific interstate circumstances)
Applies to car trailers, box trailers, boat trailers, caravans, any towed equipment
Penalties: Fines and demerit points, permit may be suspended

VEHICLE RESTRICTIONS
--------------------
Cannot drive high-powered or performance vehicles (VicRoads prohibited list)
Can learn in manual OR automatic transmission
Test in manual = can drive both manual and automatic on Ps
Test in automatic = restricted to automatic on Ps
Vehicle must be registered, roadworthy, have seatbelts for all, speedometer visible from passenger seat

PASSENGER RESTRICTIONS
----------------------
Can have passengers in vehicle
No specific passenger number limit for learners (unlike P1 drivers)
Supervising driver MUST be in front passenger seat
Additional passengers should not distract learner
Recommended: Start with just supervisor, add passengers gradually
All passengers must wear seatbelts
Children must be properly restrained in appropriate car seats

TIME AND LOCATION RESTRICTIONS
-------------------------------
NO time restrictions - can drive day or night (night driving counted separately in logbook)
NO location restrictions - can drive on any road type with supervision
Freeways, highways, rural roads, urban roads all permitted
Recommended gradual progression from low-traffic to high-traffic areas

MOBILE PHONE AND DEVICE RESTRICTIONS
-------------------------------------
Complete ban on ALL portable devices while driving
Prohibited: Phone calls (even hands-free), text messages, navigation, Bluetooth, voice control, mounted phones, smartwatches, wearables, any portable electronic device
NO exceptions - even hands-free prohibited
Only permitted when: Vehicle parked (not just stopped), engine off, safely off road
Alternative: Supervisor can operate navigation, set before departure, pull over to check
Penalties: $555 fine, 4 demerit points, permit suspension risk
New mobile phone detection cameras operating in Victoria

================================================================================
PART 3: DEMERIT POINTS AND PENALTIES
================================================================================

DEMERIT POINT SYSTEM
--------------------
Points added for traffic offences
Points accumulate and remain valid for 3 years
Interstate offences count toward Victorian record

LEARNER DEMERIT LIMITS
----------------------
5 points in any 12-month period, OR
12 points in any 3-year period
Reaching either limit triggers automatic suspension
Cannot choose "good behaviour" option (only for full licence holders)

SUSPENSION PROCESS
------------------
VicRoads sends demerit point notice when limit reached
Suspension starts 28 days after notice
Duration: 3 months minimum + 1 month for every 4 points over limit
Example: 9 points = 4 months total (3 + 1)
Absolutely no driving during suspension - no exceptions
Points reset to zero after serving full suspension

DRIVING WHILE SUSPENDED
------------------------
Criminal offence
Fine: Minimum $4,000
Possible imprisonment (up to 2 months)
Vehicle may be impounded
Extended suspension period

COMMON OFFENCES AND POINTS
---------------------------
Speeding less than 10 km/h over: 1 point, ~$231 fine
Speeding 10-24 km/h over: 3 points, ~$369 fine
Speeding 25+ km/h over (EXCESSIVE): NO points (automatic suspension 6+ months), $800+ fine
Red light: 3 points, $407 fine
Stop sign: 3 points, $369 fine
Mobile phone: 4 points, $555 fine
No seatbelt (driver): 3 points, $407 fine
Passenger not restrained (driver responsible if under 16): 3 points, $407 fine
Failing to give way: 3 points, ~$369 fine
Failing to give way to pedestrians: 3 points, $407 fine
Careless driving: 4 points, fine varies
Not displaying L plates: 1-3 points, ~$185 fine
Driving without supervision: Multiple charges, court likely, permit cancellation
Following too closely: 3 points, ~$330 fine
Not carrying licence: No points, ~$185 fine
Unregistered vehicle: No points, $1,018-$1,832 fine

PENALTIES FOR COMMON OFFENCES
------------------------------
Most parking offences: No points, $83-$185 fines
Red light cameras monitor many intersections
Speed cameras operate on many roads
New mobile phone detection cameras in operation
All fines subject to change - check current amounts

CHALLENGING POINTS
------------------
Very limited grounds for appeal
Can only challenge if VicRoads made calculation error or recorded points incorrectly by law
Cannot appeal based on hardship, work needs, family reasons
Better to contest original offence before paying fine (payment = admission of guilt)
Court has no jurisdiction over demerit points - managed solely by VicRoads

PART 4: ROAD RULES FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================================

TRAFFIC LIGHTS
--------------
Red: MUST stop completely before stop line, wait for green, 3 points + $407 fine for running
Yellow/Amber: STOP if safe to do so, only proceed if cannot stop safely
Green: Proceed when safe, give way to pedestrians still crossing, check intersection clear
Red Arrow: Cannot turn in that direction, same as red light
Yellow Arrow: Prepare to stop for turn
Green Arrow: Can turn safely (other traffic has red), still give way to pedestrians
Flashing Yellow: Proceed with caution, give way to pedestrians
Flashing Red: Treat as stop sign, common at level crossings

KEY REGULATORY SIGNS
---------------------
STOP (red octagon): Complete stop at line, give way to all, 3 points for failure
GIVE WAY (red/white inverted triangle): Slow down, give way to all, stop if necessary
Speed Limit (red circle): Maximum speed, must not exceed
NO ENTRY (red circle, white bar): Cannot enter
ONE WAY (blue, white arrow): Traffic flows one direction
NO LEFT/RIGHT/U-TURN: Specific turns prohibited
KEEP LEFT/RIGHT (blue, white arrow): Stay on indicated side

WARNING SIGNS (Yellow Diamond)
-------------------------------
Advance notice of: Curves, T-intersections, roundabouts, children crossing, pedestrian crossings, railway crossings, slippery conditions, dips/crests

INFORMATION SIGNS
-----------------
Street names, freeway/highway directions, parking signs, exit signs, service signs (fuel/food), tourist signs (brown)

ROAD MARKINGS
-------------
Single broken white line: Can cross to overtake if safe
Single continuous white line: Cannot cross to overtake
Double continuous white lines: Absolutely no crossing
Yellow edge line: No stopping/no parking zone
Lane arrows: Must follow direction from that lane
Zebra crossing: White stripes - pedestrian crossing
Children's crossing: Stylized feet markings
STOP line: Thick white line at stop signs and red lights
GIVE WAY line: Triangle teeth markings
KEEP CLEAR: White text marking areas to keep unobstructed

SPEED LIMITS
------------
Default built-up areas: 50 km/h (when no sign visible)
Default open roads: 100 km/h (when no sign visible)  
Common posted limits: 40, 50, 60, 80, 100, 110 km/h
School zones: 40 km/h (typically 8-9:30am, 2:30-4pm school days), some 30 km/h or 60 km/h
Emergency scenes: 40 km/h when passing
Must obey posted limit always - it's the MAXIMUM
Adjust speed for conditions (weather, visibility, traffic)

================================================================================
PART 5: INTERSECTIONS AND GIVING WAY
================================================================================

T-INTERSECTIONS
---------------
Vehicle on terminating road (bottom of T) gives way to ALL traffic on continuing road
Applies whether turning left or right from terminating road
Vehicle on continuing road has priority
Stop completely at STOP sign, give way at GIVE WAY sign
Obey traffic lights if present

UNCONTROLLED INTERSECTIONS (No Signs/Lights)
---------------------------------------------
Give way to vehicle on your RIGHT
Vehicle turning gives way to vehicle NOT turning
Right-turning vehicle gives way to oncoming traffic going straight or turning left
Slow down, check all directions, give way as required
If unsure, give way

ROUNDABOUTS
-----------
Traffic flows counter-clockwise
Approaching: Slow down, look right, give way to all traffic already in roundabout
Give way to vehicles on your right if entering simultaneously
Never stop once in roundabout (keep moving)
Exit: Signal left before your exit, give way to pedestrians

Single-Lane Roundabout:
- Turning left (1st exit): Indicate left on approach
- Going straight (2nd exit): No indicator on approach, signal left just before exit
- Turning right (3rd+ exit): Indicate right on approach, signal left before exit

Multi-Lane Roundabout:
- Left lane: Left turn or straight
- Right lane: Right turn or straight
- Follow lane marking arrows
- Do NOT change lanes in roundabout
- Stay in lane throughout

GIVE WAY RULES SUMMARY
----------------------
1. Give way to vehicles already in intersection
2. Turning vehicle gives way to non-turning vehicle
3. Right-turning gives way to oncoming traffic
4. Give way to your right at uncontrolled intersections
5. Obey STOP and GIVE WAY signs completely
6. Give way to pedestrians at crossings and when turning
7. Give way to buses indicating to pull out (if speed ≤70 km/h)
8. Give way to emergency vehicles with lights/sirens
9. Give way when entering from driveway or car park
10. Give way when changing lanes or merging

TRAFFIC LIGHT INTERSECTIONS
----------------------------
Green light: Proceed when safe, give way to vehicles already in intersection and pedestrians
Right turn at green (no arrow): Proceed into intersection, wait for gap, complete turn even if light changes
Green arrow: Protected turn, oncoming traffic has red, still check for pedestrians
Don't enter intersection if blocked (blocked intersection offence)
Red light runners: Watch for them, especially when light just turns green

================================================================================
PART 6: TURNING AND MANEUVERING
================================================================================

LEFT TURNS
----------
1. Get into left lane early
2. Signal left (5+ seconds before)
3. Check mirrors and blind spots
4. Position vehicle left side of lane
5. Give way to pedestrians and vehicles already in intersection
6. Turn into left lane of road entering
7. Complete turn efficiently, cancel indicator

Slip lanes: Must give way to ALL pedestrians, treat as separate intersection

RIGHT TURNS
-----------
1. Get into right lane early
2. Signal right (5+ seconds before)
3. Position near center of road
4. Give way to ALL oncoming traffic (straight or turning left)
5. Give way to pedestrians crossing road entering
6. Wait in intersection for gap if needed (keep wheels straight)
7. Turn when clear into right lane of road entering
8. Complete turn even if light turns yellow/red while waiting

U-TURNS
-------
Permitted UNLESS specifically prohibited
Prohibited at: "No U-turn" signs, marked pedestrian crossings, children's crossings, freeways, across continuous center lines
At traffic lights: Only from far left lane when green, no "No U-turn" sign
Give way to ALL vehicles and pedestrians (most restrictive)
Signal left for U-turn
Use full road width if needed

THREE-POINT TURNS
-----------------
Used when road too narrow for U-turn
1. Check legal and safe location
2. Signal right, check all around
3. Turn fully right, approach left curb (150-300mm from edge)
4. Signal left, select reverse, turn fully left
5. Reverse toward opposite side (150-300mm from edge)
6. Signal appropriately, move forward into correct lane
Continuous checking all around throughout maneuver
Give way to all traffic

HOOK TURNS (MELBOURNE CBD ONLY)
--------------------------------
Special right turn from LEFT lane at specific signed intersections
Purpose: Keep right lane clear for trams
Procedure: Indicate right, move into left side of intersection from left lane, wait for cross-street green, then proceed
Only at intersections with "Hook Turn Only" sign
Not required for learner test but good to know

================================================================================
PART 7: SPEED MANAGEMENT
================================================================================

DEFAULT SPEED LIMITS
--------------------
Built-up areas: 50 km/h (areas with buildings on at least one side of road)
Open roads: 100 km/h (rural areas without buildings, less common as most roads signed)

SCHOOL ZONES
------------
Time-based: 40 km/h during 8-9:30am and 2:30-4pm on school days (Victoria school terms)
Some zones: 30 km/h in high-risk areas
Rural school zones: 60 km/h (where normal limit 80+ km/h)
Permanent school zones: 40 or 60 km/h at all times
MUST obey even if no children visible
Flashing lights at some locations indicate when limit applies
Heavy fines for school zone speeding

SAFE SPEED PRINCIPLES
----------------------
Speed limit is MAXIMUM - may need to drive slower for:
- Weather (rain, fog, ice, wind)
- Road conditions (wet, gravel, potholes, roadworks)
- Traffic (heavy traffic, stopped traffic ahead)
- Visibility (night, glare, curves, hills, obstructions)
- Other factors (fatigue, unfamiliar roads, pedestrian areas, animals)

2-SECOND RULE (Minimum Following Distance)
-------------------------------------------
Pick fixed point ahead, when vehicle ahead passes it, count "one thousand and one, one thousand and two"
Should not reach point before finishing count
Increase to 3-4 seconds in poor conditions or for learner drivers
3-second rule recommended for learners (better safety margin)

STOPPING DISTANCES (Approximate, Dry Roads)
--------------------------------------------
30 km/h: ~20m total
40 km/h: ~28m total  
50 km/h: ~37m total
60 km/h: ~48m total
80 km/h: ~72m total
100 km/h: ~106m total
110 km/h: ~123m total

Wet roads: Braking distance approximately DOUBLES
At 100 km/h wet: ~162m total (vs 106m dry)
Components: Reaction/thinking distance + braking distance

SPEED AND SURVIVAL RATES
-------------------------
Pedestrian hit at 30 km/h: 90% survival rate
Pedestrian hit at 40 km/h: 60% survival rate
Pedestrian hit at 50 km/h: 10% survival rate
Pedestrian hit at 60 km/h: Very low survival rate
Small speed reductions make huge difference in crash outcomes

PART 8: PARKING REGULATIONS
================================================================================

PROHIBITED PARKING LOCATIONS
-----------------------------
Never park:
- Within 20m before and 10m after pedestrian crossing
- Within 20m of traffic light intersection (10m if no lights)
- On/across intersections, crossings (pedestrian, children's, level, bicycle)
- Within 1m of fire hydrant indicator
- On/within 20m before STOP/GIVE WAY signs (10m after)
- In bus/taxi/loading/works zones (unless authorized)
- On hills/curves with restricted visibility
- In tunnels, on freeways, on bridges (unless permitted)
- Blocking driveways (except brief passenger pick-up/drop-off max 2 min)
- Blocking footpath ramps, other vehicles
- Double parking
- Within 3m of double center lines (unless 3m clearance for passing traffic)
- Along continuous yellow edge line
- Nature strips (unless signed)
- Blocking mailboxes, waste bins

NO STOPPING VS NO PARKING
--------------------------
No Stopping (red sign/yellow edge line):
- Cannot stop for ANY reason (except emergency)
- Not even briefly for passengers
- Highest restriction

No Parking (red sign with crossed P):
- Cannot park
- CAN stop briefly (max 2 minutes) for passengers or goods only
- Driver stays within 3m of vehicle
- Cannot leave vehicle unattended

PARKING METHODS
---------------
Parallel parking: Alongside curb, vehicle parallel to traffic, must be within 300mm of curb, face direction of traffic
Angle parking: 45° or 60° angle to curb, forward or reverse entry, park within marked lines
90-degree parking: Right-angle, forward (easier) or reverse (safer for exiting)

Hill parking:
- Uphill with curb: Turn wheels away from curb
- Downhill with curb: Turn wheels toward curb  
- No curb: Turn wheels toward road edge
- Always engage parking brake
- Leave in appropriate gear

General requirements: Same direction as traffic, within 300mm of curb (parallel), parking brake engaged, headlights off (day) or parking lights (night if required)

TIME-LIMITED PARKING
--------------------
1/2P = 30 minutes max
1P = 1 hour max
2P = 2 hours max, etc.
Must move vehicle (leaving area, not just another bay nearby) after time expires
Check operating days/times on signs
Example: "2P 8am-6pm Mon-Fri" = 2 hour limit weekdays 8am-6pm only, unrestricted other times

Metered parking: Pay at meter/app, cannot exceed maximum time even if paying more
Permit parking: Requires permit, usually residential areas, heavy fines without permit
Loading zones: 15-60 minutes, commercial loading only, goods actively loaded/unloaded
Disabled parking: Valid permit required, double normal time in timed bays, unlimited in disability bays

COMMON PARKING SIGN TYPES
--------------------------
Signs show: Main restriction, time limit, operating hours/days, direction arrows, exemptions
White P on blue: Parking permitted (check time/restrictions)
No Parking: White P with red cross
No Stopping: Red background
Clearway: Red "C", usually peak times
Direction arrows: Point direction rule applies
Multi-restriction signs: Different rules at different times - read all signs carefully
"Excepted" notations: Authorized vehicles, buses, taxis, etc. may be exempt

Payment: Meters (coin/card), parking apps, pay-and-display machines, license plate recognition

Tips: Read all signs, check times, note arrows, photo sign for reference, set phone reminder for time limit

================================================================================
PART 9: VULNERABLE ROAD USERS
================================================================================

PEDESTRIAN CROSSINGS
--------------------
Marked pedestrian crossing (zebra crossing): White stripes, must give way to pedestrians on or entering
Must approach at safe speed (able to stop)
Give way to pedestrians even if they appear suddenly
Don't overtake vehicle stopped at crossing
Don't block crossing with vehicle

Children's crossings:
- Orange flags, stylized footprints, crossing supervisor
- MUST stop when supervisor enters road or displays STOP sign
- Remain stopped until supervisor leaves road and crossing clear
- Heavy fines and demerit points for non-compliance
- Usually operate at school start/finish times

Signalized pedestrian crossings:
- Obey pedestrian signals
- Flashing yellow "give way to pedestrians" = proceed but give way
- Give way when turning at intersections (even if green light)

Other situations requiring pedestrian give-way:
- Driveways and footpaths (entering/exiting)
- Slip lanes (all pedestrians)
- Shared zones (pedestrians have priority)
- When turning at intersections (pedestrians crossing road entering)
- U-turns (give way to all pedestrians)

CYCLIST MINIMUM PASSING DISTANCES
----------------------------------
Speed ≤60 km/h: Minimum 1.0m clearance
Speed >60 km/h: Minimum 1.5m clearance
Can cross center line (even double continuous) to maintain distance if safe
Wait if cannot maintain minimum distance
Don't drive in bike lanes except to turn/park (if permitted)

Car dooring:
- Must check for cyclists before opening door
- Passengers also responsible
- Use "Dutch reach" (reach with far hand to force body turn and look)
- Serious injuries common from dooring

Cyclist safety tips:
- Check mirrors frequently
- Shoulder check before turning
- Allow extra space in poor weather
- Be patient - don't rush passes
- Cyclists may swerve to avoid hazards (parked cars, potholes, drains, gravel)
- Give clear signals

MOTORCYCLISTS
-------------
- Smaller, harder to see - look twice
- Check mirrors more frequently
- Maintain 3-second following distance minimum
- Same passing distance as cyclists (1.0m or 1.5m)
- Can filter through stopped traffic (legal under 30 km/h)
- Don't block filtering motorcycles
- Check for motorcycles at intersections (easy to miss)
- Headlights always on (helps visibility)
- Give full lane width when possible
- Don't crowd at roundabouts
- Easy to misjudge speed and distance

SCHOOL CROSSINGS
----------------
Types: Supervised (crossing supervisor), children's crossings (marked footprints, orange flags), signalized (traffic lights), unsupervised (standard pedestrian crossings near schools)

Crossing supervisors:
- Orange vest, STOP sign
- Full authority to stop traffic
- MUST stop when directed
- Wait until supervisor leaves road and crossing clear
- Serious penalties for non-compliance

School zone safety:
- 40 km/h in school zones during operating hours
- Extra scanning for children
- Watch between parked cars
- Expect sudden movements
- Children are unpredictable, impulsive, easily distracted
- Small stature makes them harder to see
- Poor judgment of speed/distance

EMERGENCY VEHICLES
------------------
Must give way when displaying flashing lights and/or siren
Move to left side of road, slow or stop
Don't enter intersection if emergency vehicle approaching
If in intersection: Complete maneuver if safe or stop clear of intersection
Don't block emergency vehicles
Don't go through red lights (unless directed by police and safe)
Don't follow emergency vehicles closely
Penalties: Heavy fines, 3 demerit points

40 km/h around emergency scenes:
- Must slow to 40 km/h when passing roadside emergency/incident scenes
- Watch for personnel on road
- Be prepared to stop if directed

PART 10: SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

SEATBELT LAWS
-------------
Everyone MUST wear seatbelt in all seating positions
Driver responsible for passengers under 16 years
Passengers 16+ responsible for themselves
Penalties: $407 fine + 3 demerit points per unrestrained person

Proper use:
- Lap-sash seatbelt: Lap over hips (not stomach), sash across chest/shoulder
- Not twisted, not tucked under arm, not behind back
- Snug but comfortable
- Adjustable shoulder strap height

Why seatbelts work:
- Prevent ejection from vehicle
- Prevent collision with interior
- Spread impact forces
- Allow airbags to work effectively
- Reduce injury severity by 50%

Medical exemptions: Very rare, require current medical certificate from doctor
One person per belt: Cannot share seatbelts, each occupant needs individual restraint
Pregnancy: Pregnant women must wear belts - lap belt low on hips, sash between breasts

CHILD RESTRAINT REQUIREMENTS BY AGE
------------------------------------
Under 6 months:
- MUST use rearward-facing child restraint
- Cannot travel in front seat (if car has back seats)

6 months to under 4 years:
- Rearward-facing OR forward-facing child restraint with harness
- Cannot travel in front seat (if car has back seats)
- Rearward-facing safer (keep as long as possible)

4 years to under 7 years:
- Forward-facing child restraint with harness OR approved booster seat
- Can only sit in front if all back seats occupied by children under 7, or no back seats
- Keep in harnessed restraint as long as possible (safer than booster)

7 years to under 16 years:
- Booster seat OR adult seatbelt
- Depends on child's size
- Use booster until good seatbelt fit (usually 10-12 years, ~145cm height)
- No front seat restrictions

16 years and older:
- Adult seatbelt
- Standard rules apply

Five-step seatbelt fit test (all must pass):
1. Back flat against seat
2. Knees bend at edge of seat
3. Lap belt across tops of thighs (not stomach)
4. Shoulder belt across chest/shoulder (not neck)
5. Can maintain position whole journey

Shoulder height markers:
- Modern restraints have markers showing when child outgrown restraint
- Use markers, not just age
- Children grow at different rates

Child restraint installation:
- Follow manufacturer instructions exactly
- Most restraints incorrectly installed
- Free checks available (Safe Seats, Safe Kids, local councils)
- ISOFIX system easier to install correctly
- Forward-facing restraints require top tether strap
- Have professionally checked at fitting station

Safest seating positions:
1. Center rear seat (if restraint fits properly)
2. Rear outboard seats (left/right rear)
3. Front seat only when required by rules

Second-hand restraints:
- Only use if know complete history, never in crash, not expired (10 year lifespan), not recalled, have instructions, all parts intact
- NEVER use if unknown history, been in crash, expired, recalled, missing parts

Penalties:
- $407 fine + 3 demerit points per incorrectly restrained child under 16
- Driver responsible for all children under 16

MOBILE PHONE USAGE RESTRICTIONS (LEARNERS AND PROBATIONARY)
------------------------------------------------------------
COMPLETE BAN on all portable devices while driving for learners and P-plate drivers

Prohibited:
- Phone calls (handheld AND hands-free)
- Text messages (sending or reading)
- Email
- Social media
- Internet browsing
- Navigation/GPS apps
- Music apps
- Voice control
- Bluetooth connections
- Mounted phone holders
- Smartwatches
- Wearable devices
- Any portable electronic device

No exceptions:
- Hands-free is NOT permitted for learners/P-platers
- Navigation is NOT permitted
- Mounted phones NOT permitted
- Even touching phone is sufficient for penalty

Only permitted when:
- Vehicle parked (not just stopped at lights)
- Engine off
- Safely off road

Alternatives:
- Supervisor can operate navigation
- Set navigation before departure
- Pull over safely to check phone/directions
- Use paper maps

Penalties (as of 2023):
- $555 fine
- 4 demerit points  
- Risk of learner permit suspension (5 point limit)

Detection:
- New mobile phone detection cameras operating statewide
- Officers can observe through windows
- Warning period initially, then full enforcement
- Cameras use AI to detect phone use

Note: Fully licensed drivers (full licence, not learner/probationary) have different rules - can use hands-free, navigation, etc. But learners and P-platers absolutely cannot use phones in any way while driving.

VEHICLE SAFETY REQUIREMENTS
----------------------------
All vehicles on road must have:

Registration:
- Current vehicle registration
- Display valid registration label/plates
- Unregistered vehicle fines: $1,018-$1,832

Roadworthy condition:
- Brakes working properly
- Steering functioning
- Tires with legal tread depth (minimum 1.5mm)
- No major defects
- Windscreen not cracked/damaged in driver's vision
- All lights working (headlights, brake lights, indicators)
- Mirrors functional
- Windscreen wipers working
- Horn working

Safety equipment:
- Seatbelts for all seating positions
- Working seatbelt buckles
- No removed/disabled safety equipment
- Speedometer functioning and visible

For learner drivers specifically:
- L plates displayed correctly
- Speedometer visible from passenger seat (for supervisor)
- Vehicle appropriate for learner's skill level
- If professional instructor: Dual controls (brake, sometimes clutch)

Regular checks:
- Tire pressure and tread
- All lights functioning
- Fluid levels (oil, coolant, brake fluid, windscreen washer)
- Brakes responding properly
- No unusual noises or vibrations
- Windscreen wipers effective

Vehicle defects:
- Police can issue defect notices
- Must fix and get cleared before driving
- Major defects: Cannot drive until fixed
- Minor defects: Time allowed to fix
- Can be fined for defective vehicle

Insurance:
- Not legally required for registration
- But highly recommended
- Third party personal injury included in registration
- Comprehensive and third party property damage optional
- Check supervisor's vehicle is covered when learner driving

Modifications:
- Some modifications require engineering certificate
- Exhaust modifications must meet noise regulations
- Window tinting must meet legal limits
- Height modifications may require certification

PART 11: IMPAIRMENT AND DISTRACTION
================================================================================

ALCOHOL LAWS
------------
Zero BAC for learner drivers: 0.00 BAC required at ALL times
No alcohol consumption permitted whatsoever before or during driving
Applies to learners and all probationary licence holders (P1 and P2)
Supervisor BAC: Maximum 0.05 (professional instructors must have 0.00)

Random breath testing:
- Police conduct random breath tests at any time
- No need for suspicion - can test any driver
- Roadside mobile testing operations (booze buses)
- Must comply with testing request
- Refusal to test treated as positive result

Penalties for alcohol violations:
- Learner/P-plater with ANY alcohol: Automatic licence suspension/cancellation
- Heavy fines (minimum several hundred dollars)
- Court appearance required
- Criminal record
- Licence suspended minimum 6 months to 12+ months
- May require alcohol interlock device
- Delays progression to next licence stage
- Multiple offences: Increased penalties, longer suspensions

Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) levels:
- 0.00 = No alcohol whatsoever
- 0.05 = Maximum for fully licensed drivers and supervisors
- 0.07+ = Learner permit immediately suspended on detection
- Cannot claim "didn't know" or "only had a little"

Time to eliminate alcohol:
- Approximately 1 standard drink per hour
- Varies by body size, metabolism, food consumption
- Coffee does not speed elimination
- Only time eliminates alcohol
- Plan ahead - don't drink if driving later

Why zero BAC for new drivers:
- Developing driving skills
- Less experience recognizing hazards
- Alcohol impairs judgment and reaction time
- Young/inexperienced drivers at higher crash risk
- Protects learner and all road users

DRUG RESTRICTIONS
-----------------
Zero tolerance for illicit drugs while driving
Includes: Cannabis, methamphetamines, MDMA (ecstasy), and others
Random drug testing by police (oral fluid testing)

Legal drugs:
- Prescription medication: Legal to take as prescribed
- BUT if medication impairs driving ability: Illegal to drive
- Check medication labels for warnings about driving
- Doctor or pharmacist can advise
- Some prescriptions (e.g., strong painkillers, sedatives) impair driving
- Even legal medications can result in impaired driving charges

Over-the-counter medications:
- Cold and flu medications can cause drowsiness
- Antihistamines may impair alertness
- Read labels carefully
- Choose non-drowsy versions when driving

Penalties for drug driving:
- Similar to alcohol - licence suspension/cancellation
- Heavy fines
- Court appearance
- Criminal record
- Minimum 6 months suspension
- Increased penalties for repeat offences

Drug testing procedure:
- Oral fluid (saliva) test
- Quick roadside screening
- Positive screen: Further testing
- Test detects recent use (not impairment level)
- Drugs can be detected hours or days after use
- Cannot claim "it was yesterday" - still illegal

Illicit drugs and driving:
- No legal limit (unlike 0.05 for alcohol for full licences)
- ANY detected presence of illicit drugs = offence
- Drugs affect coordination, judgment, reaction time
- Cannabis, meth, ecstasy commonly tested
- Some drugs detectable days after use

RANDOM TESTING PROCEDURES
--------------------------
Random breath testing (RBT):
- Police can stop any vehicle
- No need for suspicion
- Driver must provide breath sample
- Preliminary breath test (PBT) at roadside
- If positive or high reading: Further testing (evidential breath test)

Random drug testing:
- Oral fluid (saliva) sample
- Cannot be refused
- Screening test at roadside
- Positive screen: Confirmation testing

Testing locations:
- Permanent and mobile testing stations
- Roadside intercepts
- Booze buses
- Any time, any location
- High-visibility and covert operations

What happens during test:
1. Police stop vehicle (at station or intercept)
2. Request licence (must produce)
3. Request breath or saliva sample
4. Conduct preliminary test
5. If clear: Free to go
6. If positive: Further testing required

Refusing testing:
- Cannot refuse to be tested
- Refusal treated as positive test result
- Same penalties as failed test
- Additional charges possible
- Courts take refusal very seriously

Your rights:
- Police must identify themselves
- Testing must be conducted properly
- Can request supervisor's presence (if under 18)
- Entitled to copy of test results
- Can request blood test if dispute breath test result

After positive test:
- Licence suspended immediately
- Vehicle cannot be driven
- Court summons issued
- May be charged on spot
- Prohibited from driving until court hearing or period expires

FATIGUE MANAGEMENT BASICS
--------------------------
Fatigue is impairment:
- Fatigue affects driving like alcohol
- Slows reaction time
- Reduces concentration
- Impairs judgment
- Increases crash risk

Warning signs of fatigue:
- Yawning repeatedly
- Heavy eyelids
- Difficulty focusing eyes
- Mind wandering
- Missing road signs
- Drifting from lane
- Delayed reactions
- Irritability
- Microsleeps (brief lapses of consciousness)

Preventing fatigue:
- Get adequate sleep night before driving (7-9 hours)
- Avoid driving at times you'd normally be sleeping
- Take breaks every 2 hours on long trips
- Share driving with supervisor or other licensed driver
- Don't rely on coffee - only masks fatigue temporarily
- Pull over and rest if feeling tired
- Power naps (15-20 minutes) can help

High-risk times:
- Early morning (2-6am)
- Mid-afternoon (2-4pm)
- After poor night's sleep
- After working long hours
- After consuming alcohol (even if under limit for supervisor)

Fatigue management for learners:
- Practice sessions should be planned
- Don't practice when tired
- Build up duration gradually
- Take breaks during long sessions
- Supervisor should monitor learner for fatigue
- Better to end session early than drive fatigued

Supervisor fatigue:
- Supervising is mentally demanding
- Supervisor must be alert
- Tired supervisor cannot respond to emergencies
- Supervisor should not supervise when fatigued

Long trips:
- Plan route and rest stops
- Stop every 2 hours minimum
- Get out, stretch, walk around
- Fresh air helps temporarily
- Eat light, healthy snacks
- Stay hydrated
- Avoid heavy meals (can increase drowsiness)
- Share driving duties

What DOESN'T work for fatigue:
- Coffee/energy drinks (temporary, mask symptoms)
- Loud music
- Cold air
- Conversation
- Only SLEEP actually eliminates fatigue

If extremely tired:
- Pull over safely
- Take proper rest
- Don't attempt to "push through"
- Better to arrive late than not at all
- Consider accommodation if long distance

Penalties:
- No specific fatigue offence
- But fatigued driving can result in:
  - Dangerous driving charges
  - Careless driving charges
  - If crash results: Serious penalties
  - Insurance may be affected

PART 12: LOGBOOK AND PROGRESSION
================================================================================

LOGBOOK REQUIREMENTS
--------------------
Who needs logbook:
- All learner drivers under 21 years old
- Must record ALL supervised driving hours
- Required before taking probationary licence test

Who doesn't need to record hours:
- Learner drivers 21 years and older (at time of getting learner permit)
- However, 120 hours still strongly recommended for safety

Logbook options:
1. myLearners app (free, digital)
2. Official paper logbook (issued when pass knowledge test)
3. Can use both (app syncs with paper logbook)

Using myLearners app:
- Free app from VicRoads
- Learner and supervisor both need myVicRoads account
- Download app on smartphones
- Log drives immediately or later
- Tracks hours automatically
- Syncs between learner and supervisor
- Still need paper logbook for verification before test

Using paper logbook:
- Issued free when pass learner permit test
- Record each drive manually
- Include: Date, start time, end time, route, conditions, supervising driver details
- Both learner and supervisor must sign each entry
- Cannot complete logbook on day of test
- Entries checked for authenticity

What counts as supervised driving:
- Any driving with qualified supervisor
- All road types (residential, arterial, freeway)
- Any weather conditions
- Any traffic conditions
- Professional driving lessons count
- Practice in various locations

MINIMUM HOURS REQUIRED (UNDER 21)
----------------------------------
Total hours: 120 hours minimum supervised driving
Includes: At least 20 hours of night driving (previously was 10 hours, increased to 20 hours)
Day driving: 100 hours (120 minus 20 night hours)

Night driving definition:
- Driving between sunset and sunrise
- Check actual sunset/sunrise times for date
- Generally: After 6-7pm in winter, 8-9pm in summer
- Before 6-7am in winter, 5-6am in summer

Why 120 hours:
- Evidence shows drivers with 120+ hours have significantly lower crash risk
- Develops muscle memory and automatic responses
- Experience in varied conditions essential
- Many learners log more than 120 hours

Recording hours:
- Round to nearest quarter hour (15 minutes)
- Be honest - entries checked for authenticity
- Include all types of driving
- Note conditions (weather, traffic, road type)
- Supervisor details required for each entry

Falsifying logbook:
- Serious offence under Road Safety Act 1986
- Can result in: Fines up to $4,070, imprisonment up to 2 months, learner permit cancelled
- Test booking and fees forfeited
- 6 week wait before can retest
- Entries checked for authenticity and consistency

PROFESSIONAL LESSON BONUSES
----------------------------
As of current regulations, there are NO bonus hours for professional lessons in Victoria
All hours count equally whether with:
- Professional driving instructor
- Parent or family member supervisor
- Friend with full licence

Professional lessons benefits:
- Structured learning approach
- Professional feedback
- Exposure to proper techniques
- Prepares for practical test
- But hours count 1:1 with other supervised hours

Recommendations:
- Mix of professional lessons and practice with family/friends
- Professional lessons for skill development
- Family practice for building hours and experience
- Typical: 10-20 professional lessons plus private practice

SAFER DRIVERS COURSE (Optional)
--------------------------------
What is it:
- VicRoads practical safe driving course
- Formerly called "Drive Safe Course"
- Optional but recommended
- Not mandatory in Victoria

Course content:
- Hazard perception
- Speed management
- Space management
- Safe following
- Commentary driving
- Typically 3-4 hours

Benefits:
- Better hazard perception skills
- Lower crash risk
- Improved safe driving knowledge
- Does NOT count toward 120 hours
- Does NOT reduce logbook requirements

Different from Hazard Perception Test:
- Course is practical driving with instruction
- Test is computer-based assessment
- Test is mandatory; course is optional

MINIMUM LEARNER PERIOD DURATION
--------------------------------
Under 21 years old:
- Minimum 12 months continuously on learner permit
- No breaks in permit (if suspended, time restarts)
- Must complete 120 hours of supervised driving
- Must pass Hazard Perception Test

21 to under 25 years old:
- Minimum 6 months on learner permit
- No logbook hours requirement
- Must pass Hazard Perception Test

25 years and older:
- Minimum 3 months on learner permit
- No logbook hours requirement
- Must pass Hazard Perception Test

Calculating period:
- Starts from date learner permit issued
- Must be continuous (suspensions restart period)
- Must maintain valid permit throughout

Why minimum period:
- Time to develop skills
- Experience in varied conditions
- Maturity and responsibility development
- Cannot rush through to probationary licence

REQUIREMENTS TO PROGRESS TO PROBATIONARY LICENCE
-------------------------------------------------
Prerequisites (under 21):
1. Be at least 18 years old
2. Held learner permit minimum 12 continuous months
3. Completed 120 hours supervised driving (including 20 hours night)
4. Passed Hazard Perception Test (minimum age 17 years 11 months)
5. Pass Drive Test (on-road practical test)

Prerequisites (21-24):
1. Be at least 18 years old
2. Held learner permit minimum 6 continuous months
3. No logbook requirement (but 120 hours recommended)
4. Passed Hazard Perception Test
5. Pass Drive Test

Prerequisites (25+):
1. Be at least 18 years old
2. Held learner permit minimum 3 continuous months
3. No logbook requirement (but 120 hours recommended)
4. Passed Hazard Perception Test
5. Pass Drive Test

Hazard Perception Test:
- Computer-based test
- Assesses ability to recognize and respond to hazards
- Shows video scenarios
- Touch screen when see developing hazards
- Must pass before attempting Drive Test
- Can attempt from 17 years 11 months
- Booking and fee required
- Separate from Drive Test

PRACTICAL DRIVING TEST (DRIVE TEST) OVERVIEW
---------------------------------------------
Test format:
- On-road practical driving assessment
- Approximately 30-45 minutes
- VicRoads testing officer assesses
- Testing officer sits in back seat
- Professional driving instructor or supervisor can accompany (sits front passenger)
- If no instructor: Supervisor must sit front passenger

What you need:
1. Valid learner permit (bring physical card)
2. Completed logbook showing 120 hours (if under 21)
3. Declaration of Completion signed by supervisor
4. Passed Hazard Perception Test
5. Appropriate vehicle (registered, roadworthy, L plates, speedometer visible from passenger seat)
6. Supervisor or driving instructor present
7. Payment for test fee

Cannot use logbook if:
- Incomplete
- Not properly signed
- Suspicious entries
- Attempted completion on test day

Vehicle requirements for test:
- Registered and roadworthy
- Speed display visible from passenger seat
- Seatbelts for all occupants
- L plates displayed
- Centrally mounted handbrake accessible to testing officer (if no instructor present)
- Manual or automatic (choice determines licence restriction)

Test components:
- Vehicle safety check
- Basic vehicle control
- Observation and scanning
- Lane positioning
- Speed management
- Following distance
- Gap selection
- Giving way correctly
- Turns (left, right, U-turn where applicable)
- Roundabouts
- Parking (parallel park, reverse park, or angle park)
- Three-point turn or similar maneuver
- General safe driving

Common test errors:
- Observation faults (not checking properly)
- Speed management (too fast or too slow)
- Lane positioning
- Giving way errors
- Following too closely
- Parking faults
- Rough vehicle control

Fail reasons:
- Dangerous actions (automatic fail)
- Too many errors accumulating
- Critical errors (specific serious faults)
- Not following testing officer instructions
- Learner permit expired
- Logbook not complete/authentic

If you fail:
- Can rebook and retry (fees apply)
- Receive feedback on errors
- Practice areas of weakness
- No limit on attempts
- No waiting period (but recommended to practice first)

If you pass:
- Progress to probationary licence
- Under 21: P1 licence (red plates) for minimum 12 months
- 21-24: P2 licence (green plates) for minimum 3 years
- 25+: P2 licence (green plates) for minimum 3 years
- New restrictions apply (different from learner)
- Receive probationary licence pack with information

Test booking:
- Book online at VicRoads website
- Book at VicRoads customer service center
- Fees: Test fee + appointment fee
- Must give 24 hours notice to cancel (or forfeit fees)
- Medical certificate required for refund if cancelling late

Test preparation:
- Practice all maneuvers repeatedly
- Experience varied conditions
- Professional lessons recommended
- Study "Road to Solo Driving" handbook again
- Understand test criteria
- Practice in test vehicle
- Know test route area if possible

After test:
- If pass: Issued probationary licence (card sent in mail)
- Different restrictions apply (check probationary rules)
- Remove L plates, attach P plates (red or green depending on age)
- Can drive unsupervised but with probationary restrictions
- Demerit point limits change (still 5 in 12 months / 12 in 3 years)

Progression pathway under 21:
- Learner (minimum 12 months, 120 hours) → 
- P1/Red Ps (minimum 12 months) → 
- P2/Green Ps (minimum 3 years) → 
- Full licence (no test required, automatic progression)

Progression pathway 21-24:
- Learner (minimum 6 months) → 
- P2/Green Ps directly (minimum 3 years) → 
- Full licence (automatic progression)

Progression pathway 25+:
- Learner (minimum 3 months) → 
- P2/Green Ps directly (minimum 3 years) → 
- Full licence (automatic progression)

TIPS FOR SUCCESS
-----------------
Practice consistently:
- Regular practice better than occasional long sessions
- 2-3 hours per week for full year = 120 hours
- Variety of conditions essential
- Different times of day
- Different weather
- Different traffic levels

Build skills progressively:
- Stage 1: Car control, basic maneuvers
- Stage 2: Low-traffic suburban streets
- Stage 3: Busier roads, varied conditions
- Stage 4: Complex traffic, freeways, full responsibility

Use resources:
- myLearners website and app
- "Road to Solo Driving" handbook
- VicRoads resources
- Professional driving lessons
- Supervisor guide (provided in learner kit)

Stay safe:
- Never practice without permit
- Always have qualified supervisor
- Build confidence gradually
- Don't rush through hours
- Quality over quantity
- Focus on developing good habits

Common mistakes to avoid:
- Falsifying logbook
- Not getting enough night hours
- Rushing through minimum period
- Not experiencing varied conditions
- Relying only on one supervisor
- Not taking professional lessons
- Inadequate practice before test

Remember:
- The requirements exist for safety
- Graduated licensing system proven to reduce crashes
- Experience in varied conditions essential
- Patient, thorough preparation leads to safer driving
- Goal is not just passing test but becoming safe driver

COMMON Q&A SECTION
================================================================================

Q: What's the minimum age to get a learner permit in Victoria?
A: 16 years old. You can enroll in the online learner test at 15 years 11 months, but the permit is only issued when you turn 16.

Q: Do Victorian learner drivers have a maximum speed limit?
A: No, Victoria does not impose a specific speed limit for learners (unlike NSW's 90 km/h limit). However, learners must always obey the posted speed limit and drive at a safe speed for conditions.

Q: Can learner drivers use hands-free mobile phones in Victoria?
A: Absolutely not. Learner drivers cannot use mobile phones in ANY way while driving - including hands-free, Bluetooth, navigation, or voice control. The ban is complete.

Q: How many hours must learners under 21 complete?
A: 120 hours minimum, including at least 20 hours at night. Those 21 and older have no minimum hour requirement but 120 hours is still strongly recommended.

Q: Can learner drivers tow trailers in Victoria?
A: No. Learner drivers cannot tow any trailers or vehicles. This is an absolute ban with no exceptions.

Q: What is the BAC limit for learner drivers?
A: Zero (0.00). Learner drivers cannot have any alcohol in their system while driving.

Q: What is the demerit point limit for learner drivers?
A: 5 demerit points in any 12-month period, or 12 points in any 3-year period. Reaching either limit triggers automatic suspension.

Q: Do learner drivers need to display L plates?
A: Yes, L plates must be displayed on both the front and rear of the vehicle at all times while driving, visible from 20 meters away.

Q: Can learner drivers drive on freeways in Victoria?
A: Yes, learner drivers can drive on freeways and all road types, provided they have appropriate supervision.

Q: What happens if caught driving without a supervisor?
A: Severe penalties including multiple charges (driving without supervision + unlicensed driving), heavy fines, likely permit cancellation, and possible court appearance.

Q: Can you reduce the 120 hour logbook requirement with professional lessons?
A: No, Victoria does not offer bonus hours for professional lessons. All hours count equally whether with professional instructor or private supervisor.

Q: How long must I hold my learner permit before testing for Ps?
A: Under 21: minimum 12 months. Age 21-24: minimum 6 months. Age 25+: minimum 3 months.

Q: What's the difference between "No Stopping" and "No Parking"?
A: No Stopping means you cannot stop for ANY reason (red sign or yellow edge line). No Parking means you cannot park but CAN stop briefly (max 2 minutes) for passengers or goods only.

Q: Must I give way to my right at all intersections?
A: No. Only at uncontrolled intersections (no signs or lights). At controlled intersections, follow the signs, lights, or standard give-way rules.

Q: What is the minimum passing distance for cyclists?
A: 1 meter when speed limit is 60 km/h or less. 1.5 meters when speed limit is above 60 km/h.

Q: Are hook turns tested in the practical driving test?
A: No, hook turns are not required for the learner test. They're specific to Melbourne CBD intersections with special signage.

Q: Can I drive if taking prescription medication?
A: Only if the medication does not impair your driving ability. Check labels and consult your doctor or pharmacist. Driving while impaired is illegal even with legal medications.

Q: What happens if I get 5 demerit points as a learner?
A: Your permit is automatically suspended. You cannot choose the "good behaviour" option (that's only for full licence holders). Suspension is typically 3 months plus additional time for points over the limit.

Q: Must children in car seats sit in the back seat?
A: Children under 4 years must sit in back seat if the car has back seats. Children 4-7 can sit in front only if all back seats are occupied by children under 7, or if the vehicle has no back seat.

Q: Can I park on a nature strip?
A: Generally no, unless signs specifically permit it. Parking on nature strips is prohibited in most areas.

Q: How far from an intersection can I park?
A: At least 20 meters before an intersection with traffic lights, or 10 meters before an intersection without lights. At least 10 meters after any intersection.

Q: What should I do if an emergency vehicle is behind me with sirens on?
A: Move to the left side of the road, slow down or stop, and wait until the emergency vehicle passes. Then resume normal driving.

Q: When do school zones operate?
A: Typically 8:00am-9:30am and 2:30pm-4:00pm on school days during Victorian school terms. Check signs for specific times as they can vary.

Q: Can I carry passengers as a learner driver?
A: Yes, learner drivers can carry passengers (no number limit). The supervising driver must always be in the front passenger seat, and all passengers must wear seatbelts.

Q: What is the penalty for using a mobile phone while driving as a learner?
A: $555 fine and 4 demerit points. This puts you dangerously close to the 5-point suspension limit.

Q: Do I need to complete a Safer Drivers Course?
A: No, it's optional in Victoria (not mandatory). It's recommended for skill development but doesn't count toward the 120 hours or reduce any requirements.

Q: Can I drive a manual or automatic car?
A: You can learn in either. If you test in a manual car, you can drive both manual and automatic on your Ps. If you test in an automatic, you're restricted to automatic vehicles.

Q: What's the difference between P1 and P2 licences?
A: P1 (red plates) is for drivers under 21 who progress from learners - held for minimum 12 months with stricter restrictions. P2 (green plates) follows P1, or is issued directly to those 21+ - held for minimum 3 years with fewer restrictions.

Q: When can I take the Hazard Perception Test?
A: From 17 years and 11 months of age. You must pass it before you can take the practical Drive Test.

Q: Is there a limit to how many times I can attempt the Drive Test?
A: No, there's no limit on attempts. However, each attempt requires booking and payment of fees.

Q: What's the survival rate for pedestrians hit at different speeds?
A: At 30 km/h: 90% survival. At 40 km/h: 60% survival. At 50 km/h: 10% survival. At 60+ km/h: Very low survival. This shows why school zones and lower speeds in pedestrian areas are critical.

KEY PRACTICAL TIPS FOR LEARNER DRIVERS
================================================================================

GETTING STARTED
- Study the handbook thoroughly before taking the knowledge test
- Practice tests available online - use them!
- Start driving in quiet areas with low traffic
- Car parks on weekends are great for initial practice
- Build confidence with basic car control before venturing onto busier roads

EFFECTIVE PRACTICE
- Consistency is key - regular short sessions better than occasional long ones
- Practice at different times of day (morning, afternoon, evening, night)
- Experience different weather conditions (rain, fog if safe)
- Drive in varied locations (suburban, urban, rural, freeways)
- Practice specific maneuvers repeatedly (parking, three-point turns, hill starts)

LOGBOOK MANAGEMENT
- Record drives immediately or on same day (don't rely on memory)
- Be honest with hours - entries are checked for authenticity
- Include variety of conditions and road types in practice
- Use myLearners app to track easily
- Supervisor must be present and sign off
- Keep paper logbook safe - you need it for test

SKILL DEVELOPMENT STAGES
Stage 1 (Months 1-3): Car control in quiet areas
- Starting, stopping, steering
- Speed control
- Basic maneuvers
- Low traffic residential streets

Stage 2 (Months 3-6): Building skills
- Busier residential streets
- Traffic lights and intersections
- Roundabouts
- Parking practice
- Lane changing

Stage 3 (Months 6-9): Expanding experience
- Main roads
- Higher speed areas (80-100 km/h)
- More complex intersections
- Varied weather conditions
- Night driving

Stage 4 (Months 9-12): Full responsibility
- Taking full responsibility for all decisions
- Complex traffic situations
- Freeways and highways
- Challenging conditions
- Test preparation

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
- Falsifying logbook (serious legal consequences)
- Not getting enough night hours (need 20 minimum)
- Rushing through minimum period
- Not experiencing varied conditions (only driving in familiar areas)
- Relying on only one supervisor (different perspectives valuable)
- Not taking any professional lessons (they help significantly)
- Inadequate observation (not checking mirrors and blind spots)
- Poor speed management (too fast or too slow for conditions)
- Following too closely
- Not signaling or signaling late

TEST PREPARATION
- Practice all test maneuvers repeatedly until confident
- Know the test criteria (available from VicRoads)
- Professional lessons specifically for test preparation recommended
- Practice in the actual test vehicle if possible
- Familiarize with test area roads
- Review "Road to Solo Driving" handbook again before test
- Get good night's sleep before test day
- Arrive early on test day
- Bring all required documents (permit, logbook if under 21)
- Stay calm and drive as you've practiced

SAFETY MINDSET
- Goal is not just passing test but becoming safe driver
- Develop good habits from start (checking blind spots, proper speed, safe following distance)
- Always wear seatbelt and ensure all passengers buckled
- Zero alcohol policy - not even one drink
- Put phone away completely - out of sight, out of mind
- Plan drives and routes in advance
- Don't drive when fatigued
- Respect other road users
- Learn from mistakes (discuss with supervisor)
- Be patient - learning takes time

COMMUNICATION WITH SUPERVISOR
- Discuss each drive before and after
- Ask questions when unsure
- Accept feedback positively
- Point out hazards you notice (commentary driving)
- Discuss challenging situations afterwards
- Be honest about comfort level
- Request practice in specific areas of weakness

RESOURCES TO USE
- myLearners website and app (free Victorian government resource)
- "Road to Solo Driving" handbook (essential reading)
- VicRoads website (official information)
- Professional driving instructors (for structured learning)
- Online practice tests (knowledge test preparation)
- YouTube driving tutorials (supplementary learning)
- Local council maps (planning varied routes)

FINAL ADVICE
- Take your time - don't rush the learning process
- Experience is gained through varied, consistent practice
- Quality of practice matters more than just accumulating hours
- Learn from every drive - even experienced drivers make mistakes
- Build confidence gradually - don't push beyond comfort level too quickly
- Ask for help when needed - supervisors and instructors are there to support you
- Stay focused on safety above all else
- Remember: Graduated licensing system proven to create safer drivers
- First year as independent driver is highest risk - solid learner experience crucial
- Goal is lifelong safe driving, not just getting a licence

================================================================================
TOP 10 COMMON MISTAKES NEW LEARNERS MAKE
================================================================================

1. OBSERVATION FAILURES
- Not checking mirrors frequently enough (should be every 5-8 seconds)
- Forgetting blind spot checks before changing lanes or turning
- Not scanning ahead for potential hazards
- Focusing only on car directly ahead
- Not checking intersections thoroughly before proceeding
Fix: Practice systematic scanning routine, make checking mirrors a habit

2. POOR SPEED MANAGEMENT
- Driving too fast for conditions (not adjusting for rain, traffic, unfamiliar roads)
- Driving too slowly and impeding traffic flow
- Not reading speed limit signs
- Accelerating when should be preparing to slow down
- Inconsistent speed (constantly speeding up and slowing down)
Fix: Focus on smooth, consistent speed appropriate for conditions, read signs early

3. INCORRECT LANE POSITIONING
- Drifting between lanes
- Driving too close to center line or curb
- Not staying centered in lane
- Crossing lane markings unintentionally
- Poor positioning for turns
Fix: Pick reference point on car (e.g., center of hood), keep lined up with lane markings

4. FOLLOWING TOO CLOSELY
- Not maintaining 2-3 second gap
- Following trucks/buses too closely (can't see ahead)
- Not increasing distance in poor weather
- Tailgating when frustrated with slower vehicle
Fix: Use 2-second rule minimum (3-seconds better), increase in poor conditions

5. GIVING WAY ERRORS
- Confusion about who has right of way at intersections
- Not giving way when required
- Stopping when have priority and shouldn't stop
- Poor judgment of gaps when turning
- Entering roundabouts when should give way
Fix: Study give way rules thoroughly, practice at varied intersections, when in doubt give way

6. SIGNALING ISSUES
- Not signaling at all
- Signaling too late (less than 5 seconds before action)
- Forgetting to cancel signal after maneuver
- Signaling in wrong direction
- Not signaling when changing lanes
Fix: Signal early, make it automatic habit, always check signal is off after maneuver

7. HARSH VEHICLE CONTROL
- Jerky steering movements
- Hard braking (should be smooth and progressive)
- Rough acceleration
- Abrupt lane changes
- Poor clutch control (manual cars)
Fix: Practice smooth, gentle inputs, look ahead to anticipate needs, progressive braking

8. PARKING DIFFICULTIES
- Taking too many attempts to park
- Hitting curb when parallel parking
- Parking too far from curb (more than 300mm)
- Not checking properly before reversing
- Not using reference points effectively
Fix: Practice parking repeatedly in quiet areas, use reference points, take time

9. DISTRACTION AND ATTENTION ISSUES
- Talking too much while driving
- Adjusting radio/climate controls while moving
- Not focusing on road ahead
- Being distracted by passengers
- Trying to eat, drink, or reach for items while driving
Fix: Set up car before moving, keep conversations minimal while learning, pull over if need to adjust something

10. POOR JUDGMENT OF GAPS AND DISTANCES
- Pulling out in front of oncoming traffic
- Misjudging speed of approaching vehicles
- Taking risky gaps when turning right
- Changing lanes when insufficient gap
- Entering roundabout when not safe
Fix: Be conservative with gap selection, wait for clear gaps, better to wait than take risk

BONUS: STRESS AND ANXIETY MANAGEMENT
Many new learners struggle with:
- Nervousness causing stiff, jerky driving
- Panic when making mistakes
- Anxiety about driving in busy traffic
- Fear of highway/freeway driving
- Test anxiety

Solutions:
- Start in quiet areas and gradually increase difficulty
- Take breaks during sessions if feeling overwhelmed
- Discuss concerns with supervisor openly
- Remember everyone makes mistakes while learning
- Professional lessons can help build confidence
- Practice relaxation techniques before driving
- Remember: Experience builds confidence

REAL-WORLD SCENARIO EXAMPLES

SCENARIO 1: SCHOOL ZONE SPEEDING
---------------------------------
Situation: You're driving at 60 km/h on a main road at 8:15am on a Tuesday during school term. You see a school zone sign ahead showing "40 km/h 8:00am-9:30am School Days".

Correct Action:
1. Immediately begin slowing down
2. Check rear-view mirror first (ensure safe to slow)
3. Reduce speed to 40 km/h before entering the zone
4. Maintain 40 km/h through entire school zone
5. Scan constantly for children
6. Be prepared to stop for school crossing supervisor
7. Don't speed up until completely past school zone end sign

Why This Matters:
- School zones operate even if no children visible
- Penalties in school zones are severe
- 60 km/h to 40 km/h: Pedestrian survival rate jumps from ~10% to ~60%
- Speeding 20 km/h over (driving 60 in 40 zone) = 3 demerit points + heavy fine
- As learner with 5-point limit, this puts you at high risk of suspension

Common Mistakes:
- Not slowing until you see children
- Thinking it doesn't apply during school holidays (it does if during school term)
- Speeding up before the end zone sign
- Not checking mirror before braking (could cause rear-end collision)

SCENARIO 2: MOBILE PHONE RINGS WHILE DRIVING
---------------------------------------------
Situation: You're driving with your supervisor on a suburban street. Your phone rings from the center console. It's your mum calling about something you needed to know.

Wrong Actions (All Illegal for Learners):
❌ Answer using hands-free Bluetooth
❌ Pick up phone to check who's calling
❌ Ask supervisor to pass you the phone
❌ Use voice command to answer
❌ Quickly check the message at traffic lights

Correct Actions:
1. Ignore the phone completely
2. Continue driving safely
3. Pull over safely when possible (off-road, not just stopped at lights)
4. Turn off engine
5. Then and only then: Use phone to call back

Why This Matters:
- Complete mobile device ban for learners - no exceptions
- Using phone = $555 fine + 4 demerit points
- With 5-point limit, you'd be at extreme risk of suspension
- Mobile phone detection cameras can catch you
- Even holding phone at lights is sufficient for penalty

What Supervisor Can Do:
- Supervisor CAN answer YOUR phone
- Supervisor can relay urgent messages
- But learner absolutely cannot touch or use phone in any way

SCENARIO 3: APPROACHING T-INTERSECTION FROM TERMINATING ROAD
-------------------------------------------------------------
Situation: You're driving along a residential street that ends at a T-intersection. There's a Give Way sign. You want to turn right. Traffic is approaching from both left and right on the continuing road.

Correct Procedure:
1. Begin slowing well before intersection (at least 50m)
2. Signal right early (at least 5 seconds before turn)
3. Check mirrors and blind spots
4. Position vehicle appropriately (toward center)
5. Slow to appropriate speed (maybe 10-20 km/h)
6. Stop at Give Way line if traffic present
7. Look RIGHT first (traffic from right arrives sooner)
8. Look LEFT
9. Look RIGHT again
10. Give way to ALL traffic on continuing road
11. Wait for clear gap in both directions
12. Check once more
13. Turn when completely safe
14. Accelerate smoothly into right lane of continuing road

Common Mistakes:
- Not giving way properly (thinking "I'm turning right so I have priority" - WRONG!)
- Only checking one direction
- Taking insufficient gap
- Not stopping at Give Way line when traffic present
- Pulling out in front of approaching vehicles
- Forgetting to signal
- Turning into wrong lane

Why T-Intersections Are High-Risk:
- Vehicle on terminating road must give way to ALL traffic
- Most restrictive give way rule after U-turns
- Many crashes occur here due to poor gap judgment
- Terminating road driver has no priority whatsoever

SCENARIO 4: ROUNDABOUT WITH MULTIPLE LANES
-------------------------------------------
Situation: You approach a two-lane roundabout. You want to turn right (third exit). There are vehicles already in the roundabout and vehicles approaching from other directions.

Correct Procedure:
1. Early observation of roundabout layout
2. Choose right lane for approach (right turn uses right lane)
3. Signal RIGHT on approach
4. Slow down significantly
5. Look RIGHT for traffic already in roundabout
6. Give way to ALL traffic in roundabout
7. Give way to traffic entering from right if simultaneous
8. When clear, enter roundabout in right lane
9. Stay in right lane throughout (don't change lanes)
10. Signal LEFT just before your exit
11. Check mirrors and blind spot before exiting
12. Give way to pedestrians if crossing present at exit
13. Exit into right lane of road you're entering
14. Cancel indicator

Common Mistakes at Multi-Lane Roundabouts:
- Choosing wrong lane on approach
- Entering from left lane to turn right
- Changing lanes inside roundabout (very dangerous)
- Not giving way to vehicles already in roundabout
- Signaling incorrectly or not at all
- Not checking before exiting
- Cutting across lanes when exiting
- Not giving way to pedestrians at exit

Why Multi-Lane Roundabouts Are Challenging:
- Multiple vehicles traveling simultaneously
- Lane discipline essential
- Poor lane choice can trap you
- Vehicles in left lane may exit in front of you
- Requires confidence and practice

Practice Tip:
Visit multi-lane roundabouts during quiet times (Sunday mornings) to practice lane selection and positioning without pressure from traffic.

SCENARIO 5: PARALLEL PARKING ON TEST DAY
-----------------------------------------
Situation: The testing officer asks you to parallel park in a space between two cars on a suburban street. The space looks tight and there's traffic passing.

Correct Procedure:
1. Acknowledge instruction calmly
2. Signal left to indicate parking
3. Drive past space slowly to assess it
4. Check it's legal to park (no restrictions, not too close to intersection, not on yellow line)
5. Pull alongside front vehicle (about 300-500mm gap)
6. Stop when vehicles roughly aligned
7. Check mirrors and blind spot thoroughly
8. Select reverse gear
9. Check all around again
10. Begin reversing slowly
11. Turn steering wheel toward curb (usually full lock)
12. Reverse until vehicle at approximately 45-degree angle
13. Straighten steering wheel
14. Continue reversing
15. When vehicle straightening, turn wheel away from curb
16. Finish parallel to curb, 150-300mm from curb
17. Stop, select park/neutral, parking brake on
18. Check you're not blocking other vehicles

Assessment Points:
- Observation (continuous checking all around)
- Vehicle control (smooth, controlled movements)
- Final position (parallel, close to curb, within space)
- Not hitting curb or other vehicles
- Signals used appropriately
- Traffic management (giving way to passing traffic if needed)

Common Test Errors in Parking:
- Poor observation (not checking enough)
- Hitting curb
- Too far from curb (more than 300mm)
- Not parallel to curb
- Too many adjustments (shows lack of control)
- Blocking other vehicles
- Not using parking brake at end

If Space Too Small:
- Tell testing officer: "I don't believe this space is large enough to park safely"
- Testing officer will direct you to another space
- Better to acknowledge than attempt impossible park

Stress Management:
- Take your time - no rush
- Testing officer expects careful, methodical parking
- Use reference points (door pillar alignment, mirrors, rear window view)
- If need to adjust position, that's acceptable (within reason)
- Stay calm - parking is just one component of test

SCENARIO 6: EMERGENCY VEHICLE APPROACHES WITH SIRENS
-----------------------------------------------------
Situation: You're driving on a suburban arterial road (60 km/h limit) in the center lane of three lanes. You hear sirens behind you and see flashing lights of an ambulance in your rear-view mirror.

Correct Actions:
1. DON'T panic or brake suddenly
2. Check mirrors to locate emergency vehicle
3. Signal left
4. Check blind spot
5. Move left when safe (left lane or far left of road)
6. Slow down or stop if necessary
7. Don't block intersection if approaching one
8. Stay stopped/left until emergency vehicle completely passes
9. Check mirrors
10. Signal right (if moving back to center lane)
11. Resume normal driving when safe

What NOT to Do:
❌ Brake suddenly without checking behind
❌ Swerve dangerously to get out of way
❌ Go through red light (unless directed by police AND safe)
❌ Block intersection trying to get out of way
❌ Follow closely behind emergency vehicle
❌ Resume normal speed immediately behind emergency vehicle

Special Situations:

At Traffic Lights:
- If you can safely clear intersection on green: Do so
- Then pull left after intersection
- If can't clear: Stay stopped, pull left as much as possible
- Don't go through red light unless directed by police

In Right Lane:
- May need to stay in right lane if that provides clear path
- Or move left if that's clearer
- Judge based on where emergency vehicle can pass most easily

One-Lane Road:
- Pull as far left as possible
- May need to partially mount curb (if safe to do so)
- Stop if necessary to let emergency vehicle pass

Multiple Emergency Vehicles:
- Wait until all have passed before resuming
- Ambulance, fire truck, police may all be responding to same incident

Penalties:
- Not giving way: Heavy fines + 3 demerit points
- Serious offence endangering emergency response
- Following too close to emergency vehicle: Also penalized

SCENARIO 7: OBSERVATION FAILURE LEADING TO CRASH
-------------------------------------------------
Situation: You're changing lanes to the left on a multi-lane suburban arterial. You check your left mirror, signal left, and begin moving. Suddenly you hear a horn - there's a motorcyclist in your blind spot that you didn't see.

What Went Wrong:
1. Didn't check blind spot (only checked mirror)
2. Motorcycles easily hidden in blind spots
3. Didn't give enough time after signaling (should signal 5+ seconds before)
4. May have been traveling too fast to have full awareness
5. Didn't do head-check over left shoulder

Correct Lane Change Procedure Should Be:
1. Check center mirror (general traffic awareness)
2. Signal left (at least 5 seconds before)
3. Check left mirror
4. Check blind spot (head-check over left shoulder)
5. Check left mirror again
6. Begin lane change smoothly only when completely clear
7. Move progressively (don't swerve)
8. Check mirrors during lane change
9. Complete lane change
10. Cancel signal
11. Position appropriately in new lane

Why This Is Critical:
- Blind spots exist in all vehicles
- Motorcycles, bicycles, small cars easily hidden
- Mirror checks alone are insufficient
- Head-checks (shoulder checks) are mandatory
- Failing to check blind spot = primary cause of side-swipe crashes

Best Practices:
- Always signal early (5+ seconds minimum)
- Three checks minimum: Mirror, blind spot, mirror again
- Never change lanes without blind spot check
- Be extra vigilant for motorcycles (smaller, faster, easily missed)
- If unsure, stay in your lane
- "If you don't see it, it's probably there" - assume something in blind spot unless confirmed clear

On Test:
- Insufficient observation is immediate fail
- Testing officer watches for head movements (checking blind spots)
- Must check blind spots before every lane change and turn
- Observation faults most common reason for test failure

SCENARIO 8: RUNNING LOW ON FUEL WHILE PRACTICING
-------------------------------------------------
Situation: You're 30 minutes into a practice session when the fuel warning light comes on. You're in an unfamiliar area and not sure where the nearest petrol station is.

Correct Actions:
1. Inform supervisor immediately
2. Supervisor checks phone/GPS for nearest petrol station (YOU cannot check phone)
3. Adjust practice route to go to petrol station
4. Drive conservatively to maximize fuel (gentle acceleration, maintain steady speed, minimize braking)
5. If very low, may need to end session and supervisor drives to station
6. Learn from this: Always check fuel before practice sessions

What NOT to Do:
❌ Check your phone for petrol stations (illegal for learners to use phone)
❌ Panic and drive erratically
❌ Continue practice until fuel completely runs out
❌ Attempt to "make it home" if station is closer

Lesson Learned:
- Check fuel, oil, water, tires BEFORE every practice session
- Supervisor's responsibility to ensure vehicle suitable for practice
- Learner should develop habit of vehicle checks
- Running out of fuel is dangerous and avoidable
- Pre-practice vehicle check should include: Fuel level, tire pressures (visual check), all lights working, windscreen clean, mirrors adjusted, L plates displayed

Pre-Drive Checklist for Every Session:
□ Fuel adequate for planned practice
□ Tires visually okay (no obvious deflation)
□ Mirrors adjusted properly
□ Seat adjusted for learner
□ Seatbelts working
□ L plates displayed front and rear
□ Supervisor has licence with them
□ Phone put away (learner's phone)
□ Route planned
□ Weather checked
□ Learner feeling alert and ready

SCENARIO 9: BEING TAILGATED BY AGGRESSIVE DRIVER
-------------------------------------------------
Situation: You're driving at 60 km/h (the speed limit) on a suburban arterial. A car behind you is tailgating aggressively, clearly frustrated you're not speeding. You're in left lane of two lanes. The driver is flashing headlights at you.

Correct Response:
1. Don't speed up (remain at speed limit)
2. Don't brake suddenly (could cause crash)
3. Maintain your safe speed and lane position
4. Check if you're in right lane - if so, move left when safe (keep left unless overtaking rule)
5. If already in left lane, maintain position
6. Increase your following distance from car ahead (if rear-ended, prevents multi-car pile-up)
7. Let aggressive driver pass when they choose to
8. Don't engage or respond to aggressive behavior
9. If feeling very unsafe, indicate and pull over when safe (let them pass)
10. Don't be intimidated into speeding or unsafe driving
11. If behavior extreme or dangerous, supervisor note registration and report to police

What NOT to Do:
❌ Speed up to appease aggressive driver (leads to speeding fine)
❌ Brake-check them (extremely dangerous, could cause crash)
❌ Engage in aggressive behavior back
❌ Make rude gestures
❌ Let their behavior make you nervous and drive poorly
❌ Feel you must drive faster than speed limit

Why This Happens:
- Some drivers frustrated by learner drivers
- May not understand L plates mean still learning
- May be impatient or running late
- Some drivers simply aggressive by nature
- Not your responsibility to accommodate their impatience

Supervisor's Role:
- Provide calm reassurance to learner
- Coach learner to maintain safe driving
- Don't escalate situation
- Note registration if behavior dangerous
- Report to police if extremely dangerous (calling 131 444 or 000 if immediate danger)

Learning Points:
- You have every right to be on road as learner
- Must obey speed limits (not exceed them)
- Other drivers' impatience not your problem
- Maintain safe, legal driving regardless of pressure
- Keep left rule means slower vehicles should be in left lane (you're already there)
- Defensive driving means anticipating others' poor behavior and staying safe

On Test:
- Testing officer may have learner drive in challenging traffic conditions
- Handling pressure from other drivers calmly is assessed
- Maintaining safe, legal driving despite external pressure shows maturity

SCENARIO 10: PEDESTRIAN STEPS OUT BETWEEN PARKED CARS
------------------------------------------------------
Situation: You're driving at 50 km/h on a suburban street with cars parked on both sides. Suddenly a child steps out from between parked cars directly into your path, chasing a ball.

Emergency Response:
1. Brake firmly and immediately (don't swerve first)
2. Keep steering straight initially (swerving may hit pedestrian or cause loss of control)
3. Horn as you brake (alert pedestrian)
4. Only swerve if: (a) you've assessed it's safe, (b) you've braked first, (c) you have time and space
5. Stop as quickly as safely possible
6. Check if pedestrian safe
7. If contact made: Stop immediately, render assistance, call 000, don't move vehicle (unless blocking emergency access)

Prevention - What You Should Have Been Doing:
1. Scanning between parked cars for movement, feet, shadows
2. Driving at safe speed for conditions (maybe 40 km/h given parked cars and limited visibility)
3. Positioning toward center of road (away from parked cars) if safe to do so
4. Being extra vigilant in residential areas, near schools, parks
5. Anticipating children may be playing near parked cars
6. Covering brake (foot hovering) in high-risk areas

Why This Scenario Is Critical:
- One of most common causes of pedestrian injuries
- Children have poor road sense and judgment
- Parked cars create blind spots
- Reaction time and stopping distance crucial
- Speed makes massive difference to outcome

Speed and Survival:
- 40 km/h: 60% pedestrian survival
- 50 km/h: 10% pedestrian survival
- Difference of 10 km/h = difference between life and death
- This is why 40 km/h school zones exist
- Legal speed limit may not be safe speed for conditions

Learning Points:
- Speed limit is maximum, not target
- Adjust speed for visibility, traffic, pedestrian activity
- Residential streets with parked cars = high risk
- Scan ahead and to sides constantly
- Be prepared for unexpected
- Children are unpredictable
- Better to go slower and have more reaction time

On Test:
- Testing officer assesses hazard perception
- Slowing appropriately for parked cars, schools, pedestrian areas shows good judgment
- Maintaining speed limit when should slow for conditions may fail test
- Defensive driving and anticipation are key assessed skills

SCENARIO 11: LOGBOOK HOUR VERIFICATION CONCERNS
------------------------------------------------
Situation: You've completed 120 hours but testing officer questions some of your logbook entries. Some drives seem suspiciously long for routes described, or too many hours in short time periods.

What Happens:
1. Testing officer examines logbook carefully before test
2. May ask questions about specific entries
3. May question inconsistencies (e.g., "120km drive logged as 45 minutes on highway" - unrealistic with 100km/h limit + stopping)
4. Suspicious entries can lead to: Test refusal, booking/fee forfeiture, 6-week wait before retest, possible prosecution

Common Logbook Red Flags:
- Impossible drive times for distances
- Too many hours recorded in unrealistic timeframes
- Handwriting suddenly changes
- Ink colors change mid-entry
- Entries not dated sequentially
- Same supervisor signed off all 120 hours (suspicious if no variety)
- Entries done all in same pen/handwriting (suggests completed all at once)
- No variation in conditions (all "fine weather, light traffic")

How to Avoid This:
1. Record drives honestly as they happen
2. Don't backfill hours from memory
3. Use myLearners app (tracks GPS, times, automatic recording)
4. Multiple supervisors shows variety (parents, instructors, other licensed family/friends)
5. Variety of conditions shows genuine experience (rain, night, different times of day)
6. Make entries promptly (same day)
7. Be conservative with times (round down, not up)
8. Include breaks in longer drives

If Accused of Falsification:
- Cannot argue at test appointment
- Test will be refused
- Penalties under Road Safety Act 1986
- May need to start logbook again
- Professional lessons and instructor signatures help prove genuine hours

Why This Is Serious:
- Falsifying logbook is criminal offence
- Fines up to $4,070
- Imprisonment up to 2 months possible
- Undermines graduated licensing system
- Inexperienced driver let loose without proper training
- Higher crash risk for driver and others

Best Practice:
- myLearners app provides automatic GPS verification
- Professional driving instructors provide independent verification
- Honest recording protects you legally
- 120 hours accumulates naturally over 12 months with regular practice
- No shortcuts worth the legal consequences

SCENARIO 12: FAILING THE DRIVE TEST - WHAT NEXT?
-------------------------------------------------
Situation: You've just completed your drive test and the testing officer informs you that you haven't passed. You made several observation errors and one critical error (didn't give way correctly).

Immediate Response:
1. Stay calm and professional
2. Thank testing officer
3. Accept feedback constructively
4. Ask for specific areas to improve (written feedback provided)
5. Don't argue or make excuses
6. Review feedback with supervisor

Understanding the Result:
- Failing first attempt is common
- Shows areas needing more practice
- Better to fail test than cause crash on road
- Testing officer's job is public safety
- Each attempt is learning experience

Critical Errors (Automatic Fail):
- Any dangerous action
- Serious give way error
- Nearly causing collision
- Mounting curb at speed
- Disobeying testing officer
- Serious traffic rule violation
- Insufficient observation

Accumulative Errors (Too Many = Fail):
- Minor observation faults (add up)
- Speed management issues
- Lane positioning errors
- Signaling issues
- Rough vehicle control
- Multiple small mistakes

Next Steps:
1. Review written feedback carefully
2. Identify specific areas of weakness
3. Book additional professional lessons focused on problem areas
4. Practice specific maneuvers causing difficulty
5. Build confidence in weak areas
6. Don't rush to rebook test
7. When ready (supervisor and you agree), book next test

Timeline for Retest:
- No mandatory waiting period
- But recommended: 2-4 weeks additional practice
- Focus on specific weaknesses identified
- Ensure genuine improvement, not just hoping for different outcome

Cost Implications:
- New test fee required
- New appointment fee
- May want additional professional lessons
- Budget for realistic number of attempts

Professional Lessons After Fail:
- Highly recommended
- Instructor can provide objective assessment
- Focus on specific test requirements
- Mock tests helpful
- Professional feedback valuable

Mental Preparation for Retest:
- Learn from mistakes
- Build confidence through practice
- Address specific weaknesses
- Stay positive
- Remember: Many excellent drivers failed first test
- Test nerves are normal
- Preparation reduces anxiety

Common Reasons for Multiple Fails:
- Rushing to retest without adequate practice
- Not addressing specific weaknesses identified
- Test anxiety (consider additional preparation)
- Practicing with only one supervisor (limited feedback)
- Not taking professional lessons
- Hoping luck will be different next time

Success Strategy:
1. Honest self-assessment
2. Professional instruction on weaknesses
3. Varied practice conditions
4. Multiple supervisors for different perspectives
5. Adequate practice before retest
6. Good night's sleep before test
7. Positive mindset
8. Focus on safe driving, not just passing test

Remember:
- Goal is becoming safe driver, not just passing test
- Better to take extra time and practice
- Each practice session improves skills
- Testing officer wants you to succeed (but safely)
- Eventually you will pass when genuinely ready
- Many excellent drivers needed multiple attempts

GIVE WAY SCENARIOS IN DETAIL
-----------------------------

Scenario A: Four-way Unmarked Intersection
You approach intersection from South, another car from West (your right).
Result: You must give way (vehicle on your right has priority)

Scenario B: You're Turning Right, Oncoming Going Straight
You're turning right at green light, oncoming traffic going straight.
Result: You must give way to oncoming traffic

Scenario C: Both Turning Right Into Same Road
You're turning right from one road, another car turning right from opposite direction into same road.
Result: Both can turn simultaneously (pass right side to right side)

Scenario D: You're Going Straight, Other Car Turning
You're going straight through intersection, other car turning from different road.
Result: Other car must give way to you (turning gives way to non-turning)

Scenario E: T-Intersection - You're On Terminating Road
You're driving on road that ends at T, want to turn either direction.
Result: You must give way to ALL traffic on continuing road, regardless of direction

Scenario F: Entering Roundabout
You approach roundabout, vehicles already circulating inside.
Result: You must give way to all vehicles already in roundabout

Scenario G: U-Turn at Lights
You're making U-turn at traffic lights on green (no prohibited sign).
Result: You must give way to ALL vehicles and pedestrians (most restrictive)

Scenario H: Exiting Driveway
You're leaving your driveway to enter public road.
Result: You must give way to all vehicles and pedestrians on road

PARKING SCENARIOS IN DETAIL
----------------------------

Scenario A: Parallel Parking on Busy Street
Street has moving traffic, tight parking space between two cars.
Solution:
- Signal early
- Position alongside front car
- Check traffic (may need to wait for gap)
- Reverse when safe
- Allow passing traffic if necessary
- Complete parking when traffic permits
- Don't rush - better slow and safe than quick and hit something

Scenario B: Parking Near Intersection
You need to park on suburban street near intersection with traffic lights.
Check:
- At least 20 meters before intersection (measure by walking if unsure)
- No yellow lines
- No clearway times
- Not blocking driveways
- Check signs carefully
- When in doubt, find different spot

Scenario C: Hill Parking
Parking on steep residential street facing uphill.
Procedure:
- Park as normal (parallel, close to curb)
- Parking brake on
- Turn wheels away from curb (toward road center)
- Leave in first gear (manual) or Park (automatic)
- If car rolls, wheels hit curb and stop
- Check wheels turned correct direction before leaving

Scenario D: Car Park Angle Parking
Shopping center car park with 60-degree angle parking bays.
Procedure:
- Observe directional arrows (one-way flow)
- Signal intention
- Reverse into bay (safer for exiting) OR drive in if permitted
- Center vehicle in bay (between lines)
- Straight, not crooked
- Not overhanging into traffic lane
- Not over front line (blocking pedestrian path)
- Park brake on

SPEED MANAGEMENT SCENARIOS IN DETAIL
-------------------------------------

Scenario A: Leaving 60 Zone, Entering 50 Zone
Suburban arterial reduces from 60 to 50 km/h limit.
Action:
- Spot sign early
- Begin slowing before sign
- Reach 50 km/h or below at sign location
- Don't wait until past sign to slow
- New speed limit begins AT the sign, not after it

Scenario B: Rain Begins During Drive
You're driving 80 km/h on main road, rain begins falling heavily.
Action:
- Reduce speed even though limit still 80 km/h
- Maybe drop to 60-70 km/h depending on severity
- Increase following distance (double stopping distance)
- Turn on headlights (if daytime)
- Slow further if visibility poor
- Pull over if conditions become dangerous

Scenario C: Approaching School Zone Before Operating Hours
8:15am school day, approaching school zone that operates 8:00-9:30am.
Action:
- Slow to 40 km/h immediately (zone already operating)
- Extra vigilance for children
- Watch for school crossing supervisors
- Maintain 40 km/h until past end zone sign

Scenario D: Highway to Residential Transition
Leaving 100 km/h highway, entering 50 km/h town.
Action:
- Anticipate speed change
- Begin slowing early (may need several hundred meters)
- Watch for speed limit signs
- Gradual reduction easier than sudden braking
- 100 to 50 requires significant advanced slowing
- Don't enter town above limit

COMPREHENSIVE TERMINOLOGY GLOSSARY
================================================================================

BAC - Blood Alcohol Concentration: Measurement of alcohol in bloodstream

Blind Spot: Area around vehicle not visible in mirrors, requires head-check

Built-Up Area: Area with buildings on at least one side of road, default 50 km/h

Clearway: Section where no stopping during specified times (usually peak hours)

Controlled Intersection: Intersection with traffic lights, stop signs, or give way signs

Curb: Edge of road where road meets footpath/nature strip

Demerit Point: Penalty point added to driving record for traffic offence

Give Way: Must allow other vehicles/pedestrians to proceed first

Hook Turn: Special right turn from left lane, specific to Melbourne CBD

Intersection: Where two or more roads meet or cross

L Plate: Yellow L on black background, indicates learner driver

Learner Permit: Licence allowing supervised driving practice

Logbook: Official record of learner's supervised driving hours

Multi-Lane Road: Road with more than one lane in each direction

myLearners: Official Victorian Government app for learner drivers

Nature Strip: Grass area between road and footpath

No Stopping: Cannot stop for any reason (red sign or yellow line)

No Parking: Can stop briefly for passengers/goods, cannot park

Pedestrian Crossing: Marked crossing where vehicles must give way to pedestrians

Probationary Licence: P1 (red plates) or P2 (green plates), intermediate licence after learner

Roundabout: Circular intersection where traffic flows counter-clockwise

School Zone: Area near school with reduced speed limit during school times

Slip Lane: Separated left-turn lane at intersection

Speed Limit: Maximum permitted speed

Stop Line: Thick white line where vehicles must stop at intersections

Supervisor/Supervising Driver: Licensed driver accompanying learner

T-Intersection: Intersection where one road ends at another (forming T shape)

Tram: Electric rail vehicle operating on Melbourne streets

Uncontrolled Intersection: Intersection without signs or lights

VicRoads: Victorian road authority, manages licensing and testing

Yellow Line: Continuous yellow edge line indicates no stopping zone

Zebra Crossing: Pedestrian crossing marked with white stripes
