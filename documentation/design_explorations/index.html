Log Trip — ten design directions, one decision

Round one explored six layout directions; they converged on form-plus-button, so round two explored four directions where the interaction mechanism itself differs — slide, switches, conversation, rotation — with form fields and primary buttons banned. Each prototype carries a rejected in-direction variant and its author’s own weaknesses. Click any card for the full sheet.

Decision

Big Button + grid is the shipped design (27/30). It ties the Car HMI on every in-car criterion but stays comfortable off the cradle, and is the cheapest to reach from the existing Ionic screen. The Car HMI (26/30) loses only where its own evaluation admits: it is heavy-handed the moment the phone leaves the windscreen.

The mechanism round changed the shipped design in one important way: Slide-to-commit and the switch panel both beat every layout study on plate-status visibility by putting the status inside the control — so the shipped start button doubles as the status light (amber while connecting, green when ready), and the wizard’s and switch panel’s guarded-commitment thinking survives as the can’t-start-until-valid gating and the “plates not connected — start anyway / wait” fail-safe dialog.

The rest still contributes: the HMI’s tile discipline is the grid under the button; the dashboard rings stay on the Dashboard tab; the ledger shapes the history / evidence view; the map earns its place after the trip in trip detail; the conversation’s timestamped transcript shapes how recorded trips are presented as evidence.

Colour remains a live decision. The exploration favoured green on brand grounds (red is reserved for Stop and errors); the red record-convention build is preserved as evidence. If red is chosen, Stop must be differentiated by shape and position so two red actions cannot be confused.

Method: ten parallel builds from shared briefs, none seeing the others’ work; round two banned the form-and-button vocabulary round one converged on; every prototype self-verified by rendering and correcting its own screenshot; weaknesses recorded by each direction’s own author before comparison.
